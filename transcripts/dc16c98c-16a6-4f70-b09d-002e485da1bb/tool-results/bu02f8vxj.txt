     6	## How to read this
     7	
     8	Each threat maps to the controls that **prevent** it, **detect** it or **test** it, the **evidence**
     9	an auditor can ask for, and the **residual risk** that stays. Controls point to the framework layer
    10	(L1–L8) and ADR, and say when they arrive if not in phase 1. Markers follow the framework legend:
    11	📖 R*x.y* = documented research row in `work/02-research.md`; ⚠️ = inference or unverified;
    12	[design] = proposal of this submission. Codes: S-n success criteria, G-n gaps, OD-n owner decisions,
    13	GĐ-n framing assumptions, D1–D3 owner decisions in research 6.6, "Options §n" sections of
    14	`work/03-options.md`.
    15	
    16	★ = threat named in the task. The table assumes prompt injection succeeds sometimes: neither OWASP
    17	nor NIST knows a full prevention (📖 R6.1.1, R6.1.2). Controls therefore limit what a hijacked agent
    18	can reach, rather than trying to keep every input clean.
    19	
    20	## Assets, actors, trust boundaries
    21	
    22	- **Assets:** claim data (medical records, national IDs, bank accounts) of several insurer tenants;
    23	  production infrastructure (AWS, GCP, k0s, Firebase); credentials (SSO cache, CI secrets, app keys);
    24	  the evidence chain (PRs, CloudTrail, change records).
    25	- **Actors:** an external attacker who controls content the agent reads (partner uploads, tickets,
    26	  logs, PR comments, web pages); a malicious or compromised MCP server or plugin publisher; an insider
    27	  who weakens controls for speed; a thief of a CI credential.
    28	- **Boundaries:** the laptop is not trusted (GĐ-6); the boundaries that hold are AWS IAM/SCPs and the
    29	  merge to `main` (ADR-0002, ADR-0003). Agent settings are hygiene (ADR-0007). Hooks, plugins and local
    30	  MCP servers run outside the sandbox with the engineer's access (📖 R1.6.2, R5.5.5).

    60	## What the controls do not cover
    61	
    62	- **Management account:** SCPs do not apply there (📖 R9.2.6), so its small named group is a
    63	  detective control only (T22).
    64	- **Process-only controls:** raw partner documents kept out of agent sessions (T1); no agent in the JIT
    65	  or PHI terminal (T6, T14); agents never approve PRs on laptops (T7); tenant posting rule (T6); incident-session flags (T19). They
    66	  rely on training and are checked by sampling, not enforced.
    67	- **Code outside the sandbox:** hooks, plugins and local MCP servers are limited only by review before
    68	  approval (T2, T3); excluded commands (`aws`, `pulumi`, `serverless`) only by prompts (`ask` rules from phase 2,
    69	  in auto mode too), while the repo code they run may have been changed without a prompt (T1, T10).
    70	- **Laptops without MDM:** server-managed settings can be skipped (T2–T4; G11). The IAM and merge-gate
    71	  boundaries still hold.
    72	- **k0s, GCP and Firebase:** same principles, mechanisms open (G1–G3); no risk stop, no break-glass;
    73	  a leaked k0s deploy token is limited and logged (OD24), a leaked Firebase token is not detected.
    74	- **Phase 1 window:** until phase 2 there is no sandbox, only one credential deny rule (`Read(~/.aws/**)`, OD21), no
    75	  `allowManagedHooksOnly`, no `allowedProviders`, no SCPs, no audit-log alerts and no policy checks
    76	  (T1, T3–T5, T10, T12–T14, T17). Standing prod write stays until its prerequisites are in place ([rollout plan](03-rollout-plan.md#hard-prerequisites-before-standing-prod-write-is-removed-item-116)).
