=== docs/adr/0001-prod-changes-only-through-ci.md
     1	# ADR-0001: Every production change goes through a reviewed PR applied by CI
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (Options §9, "A + D2'")
     6	- **Scope:** all writes to production AWS, GCP, k0s and Firebase; incidents included (k0s and Firebase
     7	  have no risk stop and no break-glass yet, G1, G2; human GCP access is open, G3)
     8	
     9	## 1. Context
    10	
    11	Engineering wants Claude Code to talk to AWS directly; Compliance wants no production change without
    12	a named human who reviewed and approved it. "Most engineers' profiles can reach production" (brief), and deploys from laptops are assumed
    13	(GĐ-10, unconfirmed), so an agent on that laptop can deploy too. Incidents are where the pressure to bypass
    14	review is highest.
    15	
    16	## 2. Options considered
    17	
    18	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    19	|---|---|---|
    20	| A. PR → approval → CI deploy for everything ("IaC first") | One path, approval before apply, evidence by construction | Slower incidents (est. 11–46 min ⚠️ vs 2–20 min, Options §6) |
    21	| B. SSM Automation runbooks with an approval step | Approval inside AWS | Not verified that it meets the approval rule (G6); only for operations someone scripted. Kept on the roadmap, verified in phase 2 (OD4) |
    22	| C. JIT direct access for the engineer + agent | Fastest | Approves access, not the change; agent holds prod write |
    23	| D. Hosted agent with its own identity | Clean separation | New platform; does not remove the need for approval |
    24	
    25	## 3. Decision
    26	
    27	"A + D2'": option A is the default path for every production change, incidents included. A narrow JIT
    28	fallback exists only when the PR path cannot be used during an incident (ADR-0005). The owner accepts
    29	that this fallback meets the approval rule only partly (Options §9 Q2). Incident target: ≤ 30 min from
    30	decision to apply.
    31	
    32	## 4. Consequences
    33	
    34	- **Positive:** the PR, its approvals, the run log and CloudTrail form one evidence chain keyed by
    35	  commit SHA. Agent-written and human-written changes pass the same checks (S10).
    36	- **Negative / trade-offs:** slower incident fixes; operations that are not in IaC must first be
    37	  written as code or a runbook. Two write paths remain (PR and JIT), while the framing asked for one.
    38	- **Risks & how we watch them:** the fallback stays narrow only if most incidents are expressible in
    39	  IaC (unknown, Options §8). Metrics: JIT grants per month, share of incidents needing non-IaC
    40	  operations; above about 25 % (⚠️ starting threshold), make the PR lane faster or add a runbook, never widen JIT.
    41	- **Example:** Lambda timeouts at 2 a.m. The responder's agent proposes `timeout` 30 → 90 s in
    42	  `serverless.yml`; the PR is labelled `incident`; the on-call approver reviews and approves; CI
    43	  packages, previews prod and deploys with the OIDC role. Estimate 11–46 min (⚠️ Options §6).
    44	- **Next steps:** measure the incident PR lane on one service first (phase 1).
    45	
    46	## 5. References
    47	
    48	- Framework §1 and layer 3; `work/03-options.md` §1, §6, §9.
    49	- Related: ADR-0002, ADR-0003, ADR-0005.
=== docs/adr/0002-no-standing-prod-write-read-only-agent-identity.md
     1	# ADR-0002: Nobody holds standing prod write; in prod, the engineer's (and so the agent's) only standing role reads metadata
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (Options §9; D2)
     6	- **Scope:** AWS IAM Identity Center permission sets and SCPs on the prod OU; k0s, GCP and Firebase by the same principle
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	On a laptop the agent uses the engineer's AWS SSO cache, and one portal session reaches every role
    11	assigned to that engineer (📖 R2.2.1, R2.2.2; token-reuse detail ⚠️ R2.2.2). A separate "agent"
    12	permission set therefore does not separate the agent from the engineer (⚠️ R2.9 #1). Whatever the
    13	engineer holds, the agent holds.
    14	
    15	## 2. Options considered
    16	
    17	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    18	|---|---|---|
    19	| A. Separate agent permission set, engineer keeps write | Looks like least privilege | Same SSO cache: the agent can still assume the write role |
    20	| B. No standing prod write for anyone; `ProdReadMetadata` as the only standing prod role | Agent cannot write prod with the engineer's identity | Engineers also lose direct writes and raw-log reads |
    21	| C. Separate OS user or container for agent sessions | Real separation of credentials | More setup; planned for phase 3 only |
    22	
    23	## 3. Decision
    24	
    25	Option B. Prod is written only by the CI pipeline role, or by an approved, time-boxed JIT grant
    26	(ADR-0005). The standing prod role is `ProdReadMetadata` [design name]: metrics, redacted log groups,
    27	configuration of services whose secrets are resolved at runtime; no data-plane reads of claim stores;
    28	1-hour role session, the minimum (📖 R2.1.4). From phase 2, SCPs on the prod OU deny data-plane reads and
    29	direct Lambda updates except for named roles (pattern ⚠️ R2.9 #4, R4.6 #3). Option C follows in phase 3.
    30	
    31	## 4. Consequences
    32	
    33	- **Positive:** once standing write and broad read roles are removed (end of phase 1), an agent,
    34	  even a manipulated one, cannot write prod AWS or read AWS claim stores with the engineer's standing
    35	  identity (data-plane action list ⚠️ G13); k0s and GCP follow the same principle, mechanisms open
    36	  (G1, G3). In non-prod the agent keeps broad rights (`NonProdDeveloper`, ADR-0010 L1). Compliance gets least privilege enforced by IAM (S1).
    37	- **Negative / trade-offs:** during the transition, engineers lose raw logs and configuration of
    38	  services not yet redacted. Configuration values then come from the IaC code; raw logs need a
    39	  data-read grant in an incident. Process rules remain where
    40	  IAM cannot separate engineer and agent: no agent in the JIT terminal (D2).
    41	- **Risks & how we watch them:** SSO-token theft during a session could open later grants (⚠️);
    42	  mitigations [design] are a short portal session and a fresh sign-in before a grant. The data-plane action
    43	  list is incomplete (⚠️ G13). Identity Center assignment export is reviewed quarterly.
    44	- **Next steps:** phase 1 removes broad read roles only after the minimal real-data read flow works,
    45	  then removes standing prod write once its prerequisites are in place.
    46	
    47	## 5. References
    48	
    49	- Framework layer 1, layer 2 ("Trade-off on AWS credentials"), layer 8 rollout.
    50	- Related: ADR-0001, ADR-0005, ADR-0009.
=== docs/adr/0003-merge-to-main-is-the-approval-gate.md
     1	# ADR-0003: The merge to `main` is the approval gate: two people, code owners, on-call co-ownership
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (GĐ-5, GĐ-8, GĐ-11; OD2, OD5, OD7, OD9, OD10, OD13)
     6	- **Scope:** GitHub monorepo rulesets, CODEOWNERS, `production` environment, on-call schedule
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	Papaya is assumed to be on GitHub Team (GĐ-5), where environment required reviewers do not exist for
    11	private repositories (📖 R3.1.2), so the approval must happen before the deploy job. Incidents need an
    12	approver within 15 minutes, 24/7 (S7), and code-owner review requires an approval from an owner of the files (📖 R3.2.3, R3.2.7;
    13	per-file behaviour ⚠️).
    14	
    15	## 2. Options considered
    16	
    17	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    18	|---|---|---|
    19	| A. Upgrade to Enterprise for environment reviewers | Approval sits next to the deploy | Cost; not assumed (GĐ-5) |
    20	| B. Merge to `main` as the gate; `production` limited to `main` | Works on Team; the approver sees the code that will be applied | Nobody sees the prod diff before apply (partly handled by ADR-0004) |
    21	| C. More approvers for high-risk changes | More eyes | Slower; the owner wants two people per change |
    22	
    23	## 3. Decision
    24	
    25	Option B. Ruleset on `main`: PR required, 1 approval, code-owner review, dismiss stale approvals,
    26	approval by someone other than the last pusher, required checks from a pinned source app, no force
    27	push, empty bypass list (📖 R3.2.2, R3.2.3, R3.2.5); branch up to date, since there is no merge queue
    28	(⚠️ R4.6 #1). `production` accepts only `main` (📖 R3.1.3).
    29	
    30	- **Merge = deploy approval** only if the reusable deploy workflow alone references `production`,
    31	  with no ref/SHA inputs, no `issue_comment`, `pull_request_target` or `workflow_run` triggers, one
    32	  concurrency group per stack without `cancel-in-progress` (📖 R4.1.4; ⚠️ R3.6 #1, from 📖 R3.1.3, R3.4.5c).
    33	- **Two people per change:** one makes it, one approves it; the approver is always an engineer,
    34	  never an agent (OD2, OD5). L3 changes need one approver too, who must own the path.
    35	- **Incidents:** on-call engineers only approve; an incident responder writes the fix (OD7).
    36	- **Night coverage:** an on-call approver group co-owns every deployable path in CODEOWNERS; one
    37	  primary, one backup (OD9). It does not co-own process-defining paths (`.github/**`, CODEOWNERS,
    38	  agent config, catalogue) or the SCP stack, which only the L3 owner team approves (OD13); IAM-only
    39	  stacks are deployable, so it co-owns them with the L3 owner team.
    40	- **Agent review:** an independent agent may post an automated review; it never counts as an
    41	  approval (GĐ-11).
    42	- **Cross-team PRs:** one approval is enough (OD10).
    43	
    44	## 4. Consequences
    45	
    46	- **Positive:** four-eyes enforced by GitHub, not by the agent; one rule for normal days and incidents.
    47	- **Negative / trade-offs:** GitHub accepts an approval from any listed owner (📖 R3.2.7), so the
    48	  on-call group can approve another domain's PR; domain review on normal days is a process rule.
    49	- **Risks & how we watch them:** admins can edit the gate, logged, not prevented (⚠️ R3.2.2, R3.6 #1;
    50	  logging 📖 R3.2.2); alerts on ruleset changes; monthly report of cross-domain approvals. An agent could approve through its engineer's login; no detection found (⚠️). The
    51	  required-check app's hosting, key and triggers are open (⚠️ G19).
    52	- **Next steps:** rulesets, CODEOWNERS and its validation check in phase 1; the required-check app
    53	  before any agent workflow may open PRs; L3 owner team composition open (G17).
    54	
    55	## 5. References
    56	
    57	- Framework layer 3 ("Gate = merge to `main`", "Who approves at night"), layer 7 (audit alerts).
    58	- Related: ADR-0001, ADR-0004.
=== docs/adr/0004-risk-stop-on-risky-prod-diffs.md
     1	# ADR-0004: Risky prod diffs stop the deploy until a stack owner approves the prod preview
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (OD8, OD11, OD13)
     6	- **Scope:** deploy workflows for Pulumi and Serverless stacks; k0s and Firebase not covered yet
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	With the gate at merge time (ADR-0003), the approver reviews the code change, not the prod diff. A
    11	PR may not preview prod, because that would run PR code with state-write and secret access
    12	(📖 R4.3.3, R4.3.4; ⚠️ R4.6 #2). So a stateful delete or an IAM change can reach prod without anyone
    13	seeing its real effect. Most IAM, network and stateful changes sit inside domain files such as
    14	`serverless.yml`, not in separate L3 paths (assumed ⚠️; we have no access to the repo).
    15	
    16	## 2. Options considered
    17	
    18	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    19	|---|---|---|
    20	| A. Accept the gap | No extra step | Destructive diffs applied unseen |
    21	| B. Risk stop, approved by the L3 owner team | Specialist review | Often a third person; L3 team must be on call 24/7 |
    22	| C. Risk stop, approved by an owner of the stack | Stays at two people; works at night | Less specialist review; compensated by automated checks |
    23	
    24	## 3. Decision
    25	
    26	Option C [design]. The deploy job on `main` previews prod. On stateful replace/delete, an IAM
    27	change, a network or security-group change, or (from phase 2) an advisory policy finding, it stops
    28	and posts the filtered preview. The original author opens a PR (the workflow does not, as "Allow GitHub Actions to create and
    29	approve pull requests" stays off, 📖 R3.3.3; ⚠️ R3.6 #3) adding
    30	`deploy-approvals/<stack>/<sha>.yaml` with the SHA and preview hash (owned by the stack's owners and the
    31	on-call group, OD8, OD9); the first approver approves it
    32	(OD13). If that approver is unavailable, another stack owner approves; this makes three people and
    33	is recorded as a deviation (OD11). After the merge the workflow previews again on the new HEAD and
    34	continues only if the hash matches. Serverless uses `serverless diff --json` (sf-core ≥ 4.37.0), parsed by CI because
    35	differences do not change the exit code (📖 R4.4.2). A `mandatory` Pulumi policy violation always
    36	blocks `pulumi up` (📖 R4.2.2; policy packs from phase 2).
    37	
    38	## 4. Consequences
    39	
    40	- **Positive:** the person who approves now sees the prod diff for the changes that can do lasting
    41	  damage. Still two people in the normal case.
    42	- **Negative / trade-offs:** while a stack is stopped, later deploys to it stop too (the hash
    43	  differs), including an incident fix, until the approval lands or the change is reverted. One more review round when the stop fires.
    44	- **Risks & how we watch them:** preview output may not be stable enough to hash (⚠️ Options §8); if
    45	  not, compare a structured summary. Until phase 2, the risk stop is the only compensation for OD8;
    46	  Access Analyzer `check-no-new-access` (📖 R2.8.1) and policy packs follow. k0s and Firebase have no
    47	  preview step in this design (⚠️ G1, G2).
    48	- **Next steps:** risk stop in the deploy job in phase 1.
    49	
    50	## 5. References
    51	
    52	- Framework layer 3 ("Risk stop"), layer 5 (required checks); `work/03-options.md` §1.
    53	- Related: ADR-0001, ADR-0003.
=== docs/adr/0005-break-glass-is-time-limited-jit.md
     1	# ADR-0005: Break-glass is a time-limited, approved JIT grant, for incidents only
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (GĐ-8; Options §9 Q2; D2; OD7, OD13)
     6	- **Scope:** AWS production accounts through IAM Identity Center; k0s and Firebase not covered (G1, G2)
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	Some incidents cannot use the PR path: GitHub or Actions is down, the pipeline is broken, or the
    11	operation is not in IaC and has no runbook. Without a defined fallback, people keep standing access
    12	"just in case", which ADR-0002 removes.
    13	
    14	## 2. Options considered
    15	
    16	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    17	|---|---|---|
    18	| A. No fallback | Single path | Outage of GitHub blocks every fix; pressure to keep standing access |
    19	| B. Standing break-glass account, used first and approved later | Always available | Standing prod write; unapproved use, rejected by the owner (GĐ-8, Options §0.4) |
    20	| C. Approved JIT grant, 1-hour role session, command list, reconciled after | Approval before use; time-boxed; traceable | Approves a command list, not an applied change (partial) |
    21	
    22	## 3. Decision
    23	
    24	Option C [design], used only during an incident when the PR path cannot be used.
    25	
    26	- **Request:** command list, scope, reason, incident link, in a channel that does not depend on GitHub.
    27	- **Approval:** the on-call approver ≠ requester, before the grant; the on-call approver counts as
    28	  owner of the service (OD13).
    29	- **Grant:** `InfraOperator-<service>` [design name], assigned via `CreateAccountAssignment` and removed
    30	  via `DeleteAccountAssignment` (📖 R2.4.2); role session 1 h (📖 R2.1.4).
    31	- **Execution:** the incident responder types the commands in a terminal without an agent (D2, OD7).
    32	- **Example request** (illustrative command ⚠️): "INC-123, ECS service `claims-api`, Actions outage.
    33	  Run `aws ecs update-service --cluster <prod-cluster> --service <svc> --force-new-deployment`. Scope
    34	  `InfraOperator-claims`, 1 h."
    35	- **After:** CloudTrail writes in the window are compared with the list; a codify PR follows by a
    36	  fixed deadline; drift detection runs until it merges.
    37	
    38	## 4. Consequences
    39	
    40	- **Positive:** no standing prod write even for emergencies; every use has an approval, a window and
    41	  a reconciliation.
    42	- **Negative / trade-offs:** meets the approval rule only partly (owner accepted). "No agent in the
    43	  JIT terminal" is a process rule: the grant is reachable from the same laptop.
    44	- **Risks & how we watch them:** deleting the assignment likely stops only new sessions, so a grant
    45	  can run up to about 2 h (⚠️ R2.4.2, R2.9 #3). An immediate stop needs the revocation SCP, which keeps
    46	  the user blocked ≥ 12 h (📖 R2.4.4); no night-time path to apply it is designed yet (⚠️ G20).
    47	  Metrics: JIT used where a PR was possible, writes outside the list, late codify PRs (review at 1).
    48	- **Next steps:** minimal JIT with per-grant CloudTrail comparison and a manual drift check of the
    49	  touched stacks in phase 1 (a temporary deviation from D2); revocation SCP and
    50	  automated reconciliation in phase 2.
    51	
    52	## 5. References
    53	
    54	- Framework layer 7 ("Break-glass = JIT fallback"); `work/03-options.md` §4b, §9.
    55	- Related: ADR-0001, ADR-0002.

     1	# ADR-0006: MCP servers and plugins are allowed only from a reviewed, pinned catalogue
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (framework approval 2026-11-09; rollout priority framing Q8)
     6	- **Scope:** Claude Code on all engineer laptops and in CI; Papaya's internal plugin marketplace
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	Local MCP servers, plugins and hooks run with the engineer's access (📖 R1.6.2, R5.5.5); remote MCP
    11	servers receive tokens and data. Papaya runs an internal plugin marketplace (brief, Appendix A), and
    12	engineers install MCP servers without review (brief); use of Linear and Slack through MCP is assumed
    13	(⚠️ G9). An unreviewed server or a plugin update can read the repo, the SSO cache or claim
    14	data and send it out.
    15	
    16	## 2. Options considered
    17	
    18	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    19	|---|---|---|
    20	| A. Free choice, guidance only | No friction | No inventory; any server can exfiltrate |
    21	| B. Denylist known-bad servers | Low friction | Misses new and changed servers |
    22	| C. Allowlist (catalogue) enforced by managed settings | Inventory and supplier records; blocks unknown servers | Up to 5 working days' wait for a new tool (S6) |
    23	
    24	## 3. Decision
    25	
    26	Option C. The catalogue is a file in git, changed by PR under the L3 owner team.
    27	
    28	- **Plugins:** only Papaya's marketplace via managed `strictKnownMarketplaces`, plus
    29	  `blockedMarketplaces` (📖 R1.4.2); sources pinned to `ref`/`sha` (📖 R1.4.1b, link only);
    30	  `disableSideloadFlags` (📖 R1.4.2); version floor covering the plugin-pin fix of 2.1.179 (📖 R6.2.7).
    31	- **MCP:** `allowedMcpServers` with `allowManagedMcpServersOnly: true` (📖 R1.3.1, R1.3.4); match by
    32	  `serverUrl` or `serverCommand` (exact argv), never `serverName`, which "is not a security control"
    33	  (📖 R1.3.3); `disableClaudeAiConnectors: true` (📖 R1.3.7).
    34	- **Review checklist** [design]: publisher, local or remote, what it reads and where it sends data,
    35	  supplier-register entry and BAA/DPA status, no token passthrough (📖 R6.4.2), pinned version.
    36	  Example entry: `serverCommand` = `["npx", "<package>@<exact-version>"]` [design; placeholder].
    37	- **CI:** server-managed settings do not reach a CI job (📖 R1.7.10); CI uses a managed-settings
    38	  file on the runner and a fixed MCP set, because `.mcp.json` loads without a prompt in `-p` runs
    39	  (📖 R1.3.5).
    40	
    41	## 4. Consequences
    42	
    43	- **Positive:** Compliance gets an asset inventory and supplier records (P4c); unknown servers are
    44	  refused before they load.
    45	- **Negative / trade-offs:** waiting time for new tools; review effort for 1–2 DevSecOps people.
    46	- **Risks & how we watch them:** the enforced copy lives in server-managed settings, edited in the
    47	  admin console by Owners, not by PR (📖 R1.1.5); an Owner exports and diffs it with git monthly until
    48	  G18 is solved (ADR-0007). `disableSideloadFlags` does not cover `.mcp.json` or `claude mcp add`
    49	  (📖 R1.4.2), so the MCP allowlist must be locked. No per-group targeting in server-managed settings
    50	  (📖 R1.1.6). Tool description changes after approval are not covered (⚠️). Without MDM, laptops can
    51	  bypass server-managed settings (📖 R1.1.7; ⚠️ G11). Evidence: OTel `plugin_installed` /
    52	  `plugin_loaded` (📖 R1.4.6).
    53	- **Next steps:** catalogue v1 in phase 1 (may slip to phase 2 only with owner agreement).
    54	
    55	## 5. References
    56	
    57	- Framework layer 4; layer 8 rollout.
    58	- Related: ADR-0007.
=== docs/adr/0007-agent-settings-are-hygiene-not-the-boundary.md
     1	# ADR-0007: Claude Code managed settings are hygiene, not the security boundary
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (GĐ-6, Options §0.4; D1, D3; OD4, OD7b, OD12)
     6	- **Scope:** Claude Code on laptops (server-managed settings, Team plan) and in CI
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	The brief names "permission prompts turned off" as a problem. It is tempting to make Claude Code
    11	settings the main control. But server-managed settings are "a client-side control, not a security
    12	boundary" (📖 R1.1.7), local admins can edit endpoint files (📖 R1.1.8), Bash rules are not a boundary
    13	(📖 R1.2.6), and on laptops without MDM another login or provider skips server-managed settings
    14	(📖 R1.1.7; ⚠️ G11).
    15	
    16	## 2. Options considered
    17	
    18	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    19	|---|---|---|
    20	| 0. Today (brief): some engineers turn prompts off; no organization policy is described (P3c) | No friction | Prompts turned off unnoticed; no evidence |
    21	| A. Settings as the boundary (lock everything, block auto mode) | Simple story | Bypassable on laptops; high friction; people work around it |
    22	| B. Settings as hygiene; IAM and the CI gate as the boundary | Boundary holds even if settings are bypassed | Settings give weaker guarantees than they appear to |
    23	
    24	## 3. Decision
    25	
    26	Option B. The boundary is IAM (ADR-0002) and the merge gate (ADR-0003). Managed settings set safe,
    27	uniform defaults:
    28	
    29	- `permissions.defaultMode` = `"default"` (Manual), with a read-only allowlist that excludes `aws`, `pulumi`,
    30	  `serverless` (D1, OD7b; 📖 R6.9.3); auto mode stays available and is measured.
    31	- `permissions.disableBypassPermissionsMode: "disable"` (📖 R1.2.2); `requiredMinimumVersion` floor (📖 R1.1.13).
    32	- Sandbox with `~/.aws` hidden (📖 R1.6.3, R1.6.4) [design]; `aws`, `pulumi`, `serverless` in
    33	  `sandbox.excludedCommands` [design]; `sandbox.autoAllowBashIfSandboxed` kept `true` (OD12, 📖 R5.5.5b).
    34	- OTel exporter without prompt content (📖 R1.1.20, R5.5.4).
    35	
    36	Outside managed settings: the `--restricted` flag (📖 R6.9.1b) in CI, set in the workflow file under
    37	CODEOWNERS, and, as a process rule, in the incident-analysis session; a setting cannot force it
    38	(📖 R6.9.1) (D3).
    39	
    40	Phase 1 ships `defaultMode`, the allowlist, bypass removal, the catalogue keys, the version floor and
    41	OTel; the rest of the baseline follows in phase 2 (OD4).
    42	
    43	## 4. Consequences
    44	
    45	- **Positive:** a bypassed or misconfigured agent still cannot write prod or read AWS PHI
    46	  stores with the engineer's standing identity, once standing write is removed (PHI paths outside IAM
    47	  are process-only, ADR-0009). Compliance gets
    48	  one organization policy with evidence instead of personal choices.
    49	- **Negative / trade-offs:** more prompts than auto mode; a prompt for every `aws` read. S5
    50	  ("nobody can turn off all prompts unnoticed") is likely unreachable without MDM (⚠️).
    51	- **Risks & how we watch them:** the auto-mode classifier missed 17 % of 52 real overeager actions
    52	  (📖 R6.3.5); fallback to `disableAutoMode` (📖 R1.2.2) if the planted-injection test steers it (D1). The live
    53	  settings are edited in the admin console, not by PR (📖 R1.1.5); an Owner exports them monthly and
    54	  diffs them with git until a better method exists (G18).
    55	  Until phase 2 there is no sandbox: `~/.aws` is readable and the network open; in auto mode the
    56	  classifier is the only gate. `aws`, `pulumi`, `serverless` run outside the sandbox even later
    57	  (📖 R1.6.4; ⚠️ inference): repo code that the agent can edit (Pulumi programs, Serverless
    58	  plugins) then runs with network and `~/.aws`, and SSO-token theft remains possible; bounded by
    59	  ADR-0002. With OD12, Manual mode does not mean a prompt for every command (📖 R5.5.5b).
    60	- **Next steps:** phase-1 subset; planted-injection test in phase 3.
    61	
    62	## 5. References
    63	
    64	- Framework layer 2; layer 8 rollout; research 6.6 (D1–D3).
    65	- Related: ADR-0002, ADR-0006.

     1	# ADR-0008: CI uses OIDC; no long-lived CI keys, with one time-boxed exception
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (framing Q8; OD1, OD6; target S2)
     6	- **Scope:** GitHub Actions deploy workflows to AWS, GCP, k0s and Firebase
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	CI becomes the only routine production writer (ADR-0001), so it is the main target. Repository
    11	secrets can be read by anyone with write access (📖 R3.5.3b), which, in the single monorepo, likely includes every engineer (⚠️ assumed) and their
    12	agent. A long-lived key there is standing prod write in disguise.
    13	
    14	## 2. Options considered
    15	
    16	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    17	|---|---|---|
    18	| A. Keep stored keys, rotate them | No migration | Standing prod write for all writers |
    19	| B. OIDC everywhere, no exception | S2 = 0 | k0s and Firebase may not support it yet (G1, G2) |
    20	| C. OIDC for AWS and GCP; a controlled exception for k0s/Firebase deploy credentials | Removes most keys now | One exception to manage |
    21	
    22	## 3. Decision
    23	
    24	Option C.
    25	
    26	- **AWS:** GitHub OIDC (📖 R3.4.1); the prod role trusts only the `production` environment of the
    27	  monorepo (📖 R3.4.3, R3.4.4), ideally narrowed by `job_workflow_ref` (📖 R3.4.5; on Team ⚠️ G10).
    28	- **GCP:** Workload Identity Federation restricted to Papaya's GitHub organization (📖 R3.4.5b).
    29	- **k0s/Firebase exception** (OD1, OD6): CI deploy credentials only, until it is checked whether they
    30	  can use OIDC. Conditions [design]: a `production` environment secret, never a repository secret
    31	  (📖 R3.1.2, R3.1.3); GitHub-hosted runners, because on self-hosted runners environment secrets act
    32	  like repository secrets (📖 R3.1.5); if k0s needs a self-hosted runner, it is dedicated to the
    33	  `production` deploy and the residual risk is recorded (⚠️ G15); a single source of truth; owner, expiry, rotation.
    34	- `GITHUB_TOKEN` default "restricted" (📖 R3.5.3); actions pinned by lockfile or full SHA (📖 R3.5.5).
    35	
    36	## 4. Consequences
    37	
    38	- **Positive:** no stored AWS or GCP keys to leak (S2); every deploy credential is tied to a reviewed
    39	  workflow on `main`.
    40	- **Negative / trade-offs:** one-time migration; slower action updates. The exception remains until
    41	  G1/G2 are solved.
    42	- **Risks & how we watch them:** admins can change or delete the environment, and a deleted
    43	  environment comes back without protection (⚠️ R3.1.4); alerts and a settings comparison cover it.
    44	  The GitHub Apps used for CI agents and the required check have their own keys (⚠️ G19).
    45	  Runtime credentials inside the cluster are out of the exception (OD6).
    46	- **Next steps:** OIDC and removal of stored keys in phase 1; k0s/Firebase credentials moved to
    47	  environment secrets before standing prod write is removed.
    48	
    49	## 5. References
    50	
    51	- Framework layer 1 ("CI → AWS", "CI → GCP"), layer 5 ("Credentials").
    52	- Related: ADR-0001.

     1	# ADR-0009: The coding agent stays outside PHI; real-data reads are approved per tenant
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (research 1.9, 5.8 "not extended"; GĐ-9, GĐ-14)
     6	- **Scope:** coding-agent sessions on laptops and in CI; production stores of all insurer tenants
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	Papaya holds medical records, national IDs and bank accounts for several insurer tenants in
    11	different countries. Claude Team is not shown as BAA-covered (⚠️ R1.11.3.4). Partner-uploaded claim
    12	documents are both PHI and a prompt-injection vector (research 1.11.4 L6). Neither OWASP nor NIST
    13	knows a full prevention for prompt injection (📖 R6.1.1, R6.1.2).
    14	
    15	## 2. Options considered
    16	
    17	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    18	|---|---|---|
    19	| A. Agent reads prod data freely | Fastest debugging | PHI in transcripts; injection can exfiltrate; no BAA |
    20	| B. Agent never reads any customer data | Simplest rule | Blocks legitimate debugging of non-PHI data |
    21	| C. Agent outside PHI; other personal data case by case under an approved, per-tenant grant | Matches the owner's boundary; debugging still possible | Process controls on some paths |
    22	
    23	## 3. Decision
    24	
    25	Option C.
    26	
    27	- **PHI:** never in an agent session. A human reads in a terminal without an agent and gives the
    28	  agent a redacted summary. A store that holds PHI counts as PHI unless the agent reads a view without
    29	  it [design].
    30	- **Other personal data:** readable within an approved request naming one tenant, purpose, store,
    31	  window and agreement (BAA/DPA, partner contract) (GĐ-9); approved by an approver ≠ requester and
    32	  co-approved by the tenant's data owner [design]; data-read role, 1 h [design]. The same request
    33	  applies when a human reads PHI. At night the
    34	  on-call approver approves alone and the data owner reviews the grant the next working day [design].
    35	- **Partner documents:** reproduced with synthetic documents or de-identified first [design].
    36	- **Non-prod:** synthetic or masked only (GĐ-14); no prod copies (S11).
    37	
    38	This is a target, not a guarantee: "no agent in the PHI terminal" is a process rule [design] with
    39	the same weakness as D2, since the grant is reachable from the same laptop.
    40	
    41	## 4. Consequences
    42	
    43	- **Positive:** "minimum necessary" (📖 R1.11.1.8) enforced by IAM for AWS stores (fully from phase 2); one record per
    44	  exception (S9). Assumes injection succeeds and limits what it can reach.
    45	- **Negative / trade-offs:** debugging with redacted or synthetic data; a request for each real read.
    46	- **Risks & how we watch them:** of the 17 paths to PHI (research 1.11.4), tickets, chat, pasted text,
    47	  admin pages, CI logs, prod AI traces and fixtures are covered by process only; k0s and GCP stores follow the same principle
    48	  with mechanisms still open (G1, G3). Per-tenant scoping through session tags (📖 R2.1.8) is
    49	  unresolved, because tags come from user attributes (⚠️). During a data-read window a stolen SSO
    50	  token could open the grant (⚠️, ADR-0007). Data-plane deny actions are incomplete (⚠️ G13). Planted-injection test in
    51	  phase 3.
    52	- **Next steps:** non-prod data scan and the minimal real-data read flow in phase 1; data-plane SCP in
    53	  phase 2.
    54	
    55	## 5. References
    56	
    57	- Framework layer 6; layer 1 (SCPs); research 1.9, 1.11.4, 5.8.
    58	- Related: ADR-0002, ADR-0006.
=== docs/adr/0010-risk-level-decides-who-approves.md
     1	# ADR-0010: Risk level decides who approves, not how many; non-prod needs no approval
     2	
     3	- **Status:** Accepted
     4	- **Date:** 2026-11-09
     5	- **Decided by:** owner of this submission (GĐ-14; OD2, OD3, OD8, OD9, OD13)
     6	- **Scope:** every change made by engineers and their agents, from non-prod to production
     7	
     8	## 1. Context
     9	
    10	Engineering says "Let Claude Code talk to AWS directly" (brief); the need is speed. Compliance's
    11	sentence covers production writes only (framing §3.1). A single rule for all changes either slows non-prod for
    12	no gain or leaves high-risk prod changes with too light a review. The owner also wants every prod
    13	change to need two people, no more by default: one who makes it and one who approves it (OD2).
    14	
    15	## 2. Options considered
    16	
    17	| Option | Pros | Cons |
    18	|---|---|---|
    19	| A. Same approval for everything | Simple | Non-prod loop slowed for no gain; high-risk changes get no extra scrutiny |
    20	| B. More approvers as risk grows (e.g. two for L3) | More eyes on dangerous changes | Breaks the two-person rule; slower incidents |
    21	| C. Risk level narrows who may approve, plus a risk stop; non-prod free | Fast where damage is small; specialist owners where it is large | One reviewer for high-risk changes can miss things |
    22	
    23	## 3. Decision
    24	
    25	Option C [design levels]:
    26	
    27	| Level | What | Approval |
    28	|---|---|---|
    29	| L0 | Reads: non-prod anything, prod metadata | none |
    30	| L1 | Changes in non-prod (synthetic or masked data only, GĐ-14) | none, author only |
    31	| L2 | Routine prod change, e.g. Lambda memory 1024 → 2048 MB | 1 code owner ≠ author, ≠ last pusher |
    32	| L3 | IAM/SCP, stateful replace/delete, network, workflow and agent-config files, catalogue, advisory policy finding (full list: framework layer 5) | 1 owner of that path |
    33	| L3-JIT | Break-glass in an incident | on-call approver ≠ requester (ADR-0005) |
    34	| L3-data | Real customer data read | approver ≠ requester + agreement; data-owner co-approval [design] (ADR-0009) |
    35	
    36	`CLAUDE.md` and the other agent-configuration files are L3, because they define the workflow for the
    37	whole team (OD3). Process-defining paths and the SCP stack are owned by the L3 owner team; IAM or
    38	stateful changes inside a domain file are approved by that stack's owners (OD8, OD13). At every level,
    39	the risk stop applies to any deploy whose prod diff is risky (ADR-0004).
    40	
    41	## 4. Consequences
    42	
    43	- **Positive:** a free non-prod loop for engineers and agents (S4); the same two-person rule for every
    44	  prod change, incidents included (three people only by recorded deviation, OD11). Compliance gets a written rule for who may approve what.
    45	- **Negative / trade-offs:** one approver for high-risk changes (owner's trade-off for speed);
    46	  `CLAUDE.md` edits wait for the L3 owner team, not the domain team that edits them most.
    47	- **Risks & how we watch them:** the free loop holds only if non-prod has no real data; a non-prod
    48	  store not yet scanned follows the L3-data rule until scanned (S11). A risky diff hidden in an L2 PR is
    49	  caught by the risk stop, not by the level (Pulumi and Serverless stacks only;
    50	  k0s and Firebase have none yet, G1, G2).
    51	- **Next steps:** non-prod data scan and CODEOWNERS with the L3 path list in phase 1; composition of
    52	  the L3 owner team is open (G17).
    53	
    54	## 5. References
    55	
    56	- Framework layer 3 (levels table), layer 5 (canonical L3 path list), layer 6 (non-prod).
    57	- Related: ADR-0003, ADR-0004, ADR-0005, ADR-0009.
