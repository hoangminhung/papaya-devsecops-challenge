     1	# Framework: coding agents for DevSecOps at Papaya
     2	
     3	> Status: draft v1, for owner review · Updated: 2026-11-09
     4	> Audience: a panel of Engineering and Compliance. Every layer states what it costs engineers and
     5	> what it gives Compliance.
     6	
     7	## How to read this
     8	
     9	The framework has eight layers. Each layer has three parts:
    10	
    11	- **Principle**: the rule, independent of any tool. It still holds if a tool changes.
    12	- **On Papaya's stack**: how the rule is applied with Pulumi, Serverless Framework, GitHub
    13	  Actions, AWS, GCP, k0s and Claude Code.
    14	- **Audit evidence**: the records the layer produces, so an auditor can test it without asking
    15	  anyone.
    16	
    17	Markers (see `CLAUDE.md` Rule 1):
    18	
    19	| Marker | Meaning |
    20	|---|---|
    21	| 📖 R*x.y* | Documented behaviour. R*x.y* is the row in `work/02-research.md` that quotes the official page and links it. |
    22	| ✅ | Checked locally in this repository. |
    23	| ⚠️ | Assumption, inference or open question. Not verified. |
    24	| [design] | A mechanism I propose. It is built from documented parts, but nobody has tested it. Treat it as ⚠️. |
    25	
    26	Other references: **GĐ-n** = owner-confirmed assumption n in `work/01-problem-framing.md` §4;
    27	**S-n** = success criterion n (same file, §5); **P-n / A-n** = problems (same file, §1);
    28	**D1–D3** = owner decisions in `work/02-research.md` 6.6; **Options §n** = `work/03-options.md`.

    32	## 1. The decision in one page
    33	
    34	**The conflict is narrow.** Engineering and Compliance disagree only about *writes to
    35	production*. Reading metadata and everything in non-prod are outside Compliance's sentence
    36	(framing §3.1). Reading real customer data is a separate question (layer 6).
    37	
    38	**Chosen approach: "A + D2'"** (Options §9, owner decision 2026-11-09):
    39	
    40	1. **Every production change is a pull request**, approved by a named human who is not the author,
    41	   and applied by the CI pipeline. This holds during incidents too ("IaC first").
    42	2. **A narrow fallback**: when the PR path cannot be used (the operation is not expressible in IaC,
    43	   or GitHub Actions is down), a human gets a short, approved just-in-time (JIT) permission. The
    44	   approver approves an exact list of commands. A human runs them in a terminal without an agent.
    45	   Afterwards a job reconciles CloudTrail with the list, and a PR codifies the change.
    46	3. **The agent never holds production write access**, on a laptop or in CI.
    47	
    48	What each side gets:
    49	
    50	| Engineering gets | Compliance gets |
    51	|---|---|
    52	| A free loop in non-prod: the agent reads, changes and deploys without approval (GĐ-14 makes this safe). | Every prod change approved **before** apply by a named human ≠ author (GĐ-8, GĐ-11), enforced by GitHub rulesets and AWS IAM, which the agent cannot edit. |
    53	| Read-only prod metadata for the agent (config, metrics, redacted logs). | No standing prod write for any human or agent (S1); no long-lived keys in CI (S2). |
    54	| Self-service: open a PR, get one approval, ship the same day (S4: wait ≤ 1 h in working hours). | One evidence chain per change, kept ≥ 6 years (📖 R5.2.9, R5.7 #2). |
    55	| An incident lane: approval wait ≤ 15 min 24/7 (S7), decision-to-apply ≤ 30 min (Options §9 Q3). | Agent-side controls (managed settings, catalogue) as a second layer, not as the control of record. |
    56	
    57	**Where the design does not fully meet Compliance's sentence (stated openly):**
    58	
    59	- **The JIT fallback meets it only partly.** The approver approves a command list, not an applied
    60	  change, and "the human, not the agent, ran it" is a process rule (Options §5.1). The owner
    61	  accepted this (Options §9 Q2). It is measured (layer 7).
    62	- **On the GitHub Team plan nobody sees the prod diff before apply.** The reviewer sees the code diff
    63	  and a non-prod preview (📖 R4.6 #1, R3.1.2). The deploy job stops on risky prod diffs and asks for a
    64	  second approval [design].
    65	
    66	**Cross-cutting rules** (from the owner's balance principle, `work/02-research.md` 6.8):
    67	
    68	1. **The boundary is IAM and the CI gate.** Agent-side controls lower how often things go wrong;
    69	   they do not decide what is possible (📖 R1.1.7, R1.8 #1).
    70	2. **Assume prompt injection succeeds.** No source claims a full prevention (📖 R6.1.1, R6.1.2).
    71	   Design so that a hijacked agent can do little.
    72	3. **Friction where damage is large, none where it is small.** If the safe path is slower than the
    73	   shortcut, people take the shortcut (P3b). So friction is measured and has a time-boxed escape.
    74	4. **Nothing standing.** No standing prod write, no long-lived credentials.
    75	5. **Evidence by construction.** The path itself produces the records; nobody writes them afterwards.
    76	
    77	### Roles
    78	
    79	| Role | Who | Rule |
    80	|---|---|---|
    81	| Author | The engineer, and the agent working for them | Never approves their own change. An agent never approves (GĐ-11). |
    82	| Approver | A named human with GitHub write access, listed in CODEOWNERS | ≠ author, ≠ last pusher, ≠ the person who triggered an app-authored PR (📖 R3.2.3, R3.3.4). |
    83	| On-call approver | Primary + backup, 24/7, named in the on-call schedule | Same rule as Approver. Page target: approval wait ≤ 15 min (S7). |
    84	| Platform approver | Platform team member | Second approval for L3 changes (layer 3). |
    85	| Control owner | DevSecOps (1–2 people, GĐ-1) | Owns the policy files and metrics. Is **not** the only approver. |

    89	## Layer 1 — Identity and access
    90	
    91	**Principle**
    92	
    93	- Every action traces to one named human. An agent acts for a human; it is never an anonymous
    94	  shared identity.
    95	- Nobody holds standing write access to production. Production is written by the pipeline role,
    96	  or, in the fallback, by an approved, time-boxed human grant.
    97	- Production reads default to metadata. Reading stores that hold customer data is blocked by a
    98	  guardrail the agent cannot edit.
    99	- No long-lived credentials anywhere. Prod and non-prod are separate boundaries.
   100	
   101	**On Papaya's stack**
   102	
   103	- **Why the human's access is the agent's access.** On a laptop, the agent uses the engineer's
   104	  AWS SSO cache, and that cache reaches every role assigned to the engineer (📖 R2.2.1, R2.2.2).
   105	  A separate "agent" permission set does not separate them (📖 R2.9 #1). So the control is what
   106	  the **engineer** holds.
   107	- **AWS IAM Identity Center permission sets** (📖 R2.1.1):
   108	  - `ProdReadMetadata`: read config, metrics and redacted log groups in prod; session duration 1 h,
   109	    the minimum (📖 R2.1.4).
   110	  - `NonProdDeveloper`: broad rights in non-prod accounts only.
   111	  - **No standing prod-write permission set** is assigned to anyone.
   112	  - `InfraOperator-<service>`: exists, but is assigned only by the JIT flow (layer 7). Tooling
   113	    options: a partner product, the TEAM sample, or a small flow on `CreateAccountAssignment` /
   114	    `DeleteAccountAssignment` (📖 R2.4.1, R2.4.1b, R2.4.2).
   115	- **Guardrails in the prod OU (SCPs)**, which member-account principals cannot edit (📖 R2.3.1, R2.3.2):
   116	  - deny data-plane reads on stores that hold claim data (for example `s3:GetObject` on the claim
   117	    documents bucket, `sqs:ReceiveMessage`, `states:GetExecutionHistory`), except application roles
   118	    and the approved data-read role, selected by `aws:PrincipalArn` (📖 R2.3.8; pattern from R2.9 #4, ⚠️ untested);
   119	  - deny `lambda:UpdateFunctionCode` and `lambda:UpdateFunctionConfiguration` except the
   120	    CloudFormation `deploymentRole` and the named JIT roles. This closes `serverless deploy
   121	    function` (📖 R4.4.4, R4.6 #3; SCP vs IAM not yet chosen, ⚠️);
   122	  - a pre-deployed revocation Deny on `identitystore:userId` (📖 R2.4.4);
   123	  - protect the organization trail and log archive (📖 R2.6.2).
   124	  Limits: 10 SCPs per target, 10,240 characters each, nothing applies to the management account
   125	  (📖 R2.3.2, R2.3.4).
   126	- **Sessions:** set the access-portal session length on purpose; the IdP session can silently
   127	  extend it (📖 R2.1.5, R2.1.6). MFA lives in the external IdP if one is used (📖 R2.1.7).
   128	- **CI → AWS:** GitHub OIDC, no stored keys (📖 R3.4.1). The prod deploy role trusts only
   129	  `repo:<owner>@<owner-id>/<repo>@<repo-id>:environment:production` (immutable `sub` format, 📖 R3.4.4),
   130	  customised to include `job_workflow_ref` of the reusable deploy workflow (📖 R3.4.5).
   131	  Non-prod roles trust non-prod environments or `pull_request` (📖 R4.6 #2).
   132	- **CI → GCP:** Workload Identity Federation with an attribute condition on Papaya's GitHub
   133	  organization, which Google requires (📖 R3.4.5b). GCP can test `job_workflow_ref` directly
   134	  (⚠️ inference in R3.4.5b).
   135	- **Humans → GCP, k0s, Firebase:** the same principles apply (no standing prod write, metadata-only
   136	  reads, federation). The mechanisms are **not researched** (⚠️ gap G1–G3 below). Interim rule: no
   137	  admin kubeconfig and no GCP owner role on laptops; prod deploys to k0s and Firebase run only from CI.
   138	
   139	**Audit evidence**
   140	
   141	- Export of Identity Center assignments showing no standing prod-write assignment (S1).
   142	- CloudTrail `CreateAccountAssignment` / `DeleteAccountAssignment` for each JIT grant (📖 R2.4.2),
   143	  linked to its approval record by request ID [design].
   144	- SCP and trust-policy text in Pulumi code: version history = change history.
   145	- IAM Access Analyzer `check-no-new-access` result on every PR that changes a policy (📖 R2.8.1).
   146	- Quarterly access-review record (who holds which permission set).
   147	
   148	**Cost / value.** Engineers lose standing prod write; in exchange they keep fast prod reads and a
   149	free non-prod loop. Compliance gets least privilege that is enforced, not promised.

   153	## Layer 2 — Agent runtime controls
   154	
   155	**Principle**
   156	
   157	- Agent-side controls are a hygiene layer: they cut mistakes, they are not the boundary. The docs
   158	  call managed settings "a client-side control, not a security boundary" (📖 R1.1.7).
   159	- They are set centrally, the same for everyone, and versioned in git.
   160	- Safe defaults, with a measured escape hatch: a default that people constantly override is
   161	  replaced, not defended.
   162	- The session that reads untrusted content should not be the session that holds power (📖 R6.1.2).
   163	
   164	**On Papaya's stack**
   165	
   166	- **Delivery:** server-managed settings from the claude.ai admin console (available on Team,
   167	  📖 R1.1.5) for all laptops. Where MDM exists, also an endpoint `managed-settings.json`, which a
   168	  normal user cannot edit (📖 R1.1.3, R1.1.8). MDM is unknown, so both paths are prepared (R1.9).
   169	- **Baseline keys** (template: Anthropic's `settings-hipaa.json`, 📖 R5.5.4):
   170	
   171	  | Key | Value | Why | Source |
   172	  |---|---|---|---|
   173	  | `permissions.defaultMode` | `default` (Manual) | Owner decision D1. A default, not a lock: users can switch to auto, and VS Code remembers the last pick. | 📖 R6.9.3 |
   174	  | `permissions.allow` (managed) | read-only commands Papaya uses (e.g. `Bash(aws cloudwatch get-metric-data *)`) | Fewer prompts for safe reads (P3b) | 📖 R6.9.3 |
   175	  | `allowManagedPermissionRulesOnly` | `true` | One rule set for everyone (D1, Q4) | 📖 R1.2.5 |
   176	  | `permissions.disableBypassPermissionsMode` | `"disable"` | Removes "prompts off" (P3a) | 📖 R1.2.2 |
   177	  | `requiredMinimumVersion` | ≥ latest fixed advisory at rollout (≥ 2.1.260 at minimum) | Advisory history makes the version a control | 📖 R1.1.13, R6.3.10, R6.5 #4 |
   178	  | `allowedProviders` + `forceLoginOrgUUID` | `["anthropic"]` + Papaya's org | Closes most bypasses on endpoint-managed machines | 📖 R1.1.15 |
   179	  | `allowManagedHooksOnly` | `true` | Repo and user hooks cannot run | 📖 R1.5.4 |
   180	  | `sandbox.enabled`, `sandbox.failIfUnavailable`, `sandbox.allowUnsandboxedCommands: false`, `sandbox.credentials` / `filesystem.denyRead` on `~/.aws`, narrow `network.allowedDomains` | on | The sandbox reads `~/.aws` by default (R1.6.3). Shell commands only (R1.6.2). | 📖 R1.6.4 |
   181	  | MCP and plugin keys | see layer 4 | | |
   182	  | OTel exporter, content flags `0` | on | Usage evidence without prompt content | 📖 R1.1.20, R5.5.4 |
   183	
   184	- **Session types.** Each type has its own identity and flags:
   185	
   186	  | Session | Where | Identity | Flags | Notes |
   187	  |---|---|---|---|---|
   188	  | Daily development | laptop | non-prod roles + `ProdReadMetadata` | managed baseline | Auto mode allowed by choice (D1). |
   189	  | Incident analysis | laptop | `ProdReadMetadata` only | `--restricted --tools "Bash,Read,Grep,Glob"` | Process rule (D3, owner Q1–Q2): reads logs, proposes a fix, cannot edit. 📖 R6.9.1b |
   190	  | CI agent (PR review, code tasks) | GitHub-hosted runner | its own GitHub App token; **no** prod environment | `--restricted`, explicit `--tools`, `--setting-sources user`, hooks disabled | 📖 R1.7.3, R3.6 #4, D3 |
   191	  | JIT terminal (fallback only) | laptop | JIT grant | **no agent running** | Process rule (D2). |
   192	  | Auto-mode sandbox (day 60–90) | separate environment, non-prod credentials only | non-prod | auto mode encouraged | D1 option (c). |
   193	
   194	- **Hard limits to state openly:**
   195	  - Bash permission rules are not a security boundary (📖 R1.2.6).
   196	  - Hooks fail open unless `"onFailure": "block"` is set (📖 R1.5.2).
   197	  - `--restricted` cannot be forced by a setting on laptops (📖 R6.9.1, R6.9.1c).
   198	  - Server-managed settings are bypassed by a different login, an API key or a provider variable
   199	    (📖 R1.1.7). On a laptop without MDM, `allowedProviders` from the server is the main counter.
   200	  - In the JIT window, the grant sits in the same SSO cache the agent can reach (📖 R2.2.2). "No
   201	    agent in the JIT terminal" is a process control (D2).
   202	- **Fallback:** switch to `disableAutoMode` if the planted-injection test (layer 8) steers auto
   203	  mode, a serious advisory appears, or an audit finds auto mode touched a protected scope (D1).
   204	
   205	**Audit evidence**
   206	
   207	- The managed settings file in git, with PR approvals (change history of the policy).
   208	- `/status` and `claude doctor` output from a sample of machines (📖 R1.1.18).
   209	- OTel: `claude_code.tool_decision` with `permission_mode`; `claude_code.tool.blocked_on_user`
   210	  with `duration_ms` as a rubber-stamping signal (D1 metrics, 6.6).
   211	- Settings-change audit events exist, but availability on Team is unknown (⚠️ R1.1.12).
   212	
   213	**Cost / value.** Manual mode prompts more than auto mode; the managed read-only allowlist offsets
   214	part of it. Compliance gets an organization policy with evidence, instead of personal choices (P3c).

   218	## Layer 3 — Change workflow by risk level
   219	
   220	**Principle**
   221	
   222	- The level is set by the damage a change can do, not by who asks.
   223	- Above non-prod, one path: a reviewed PR, applied by the pipeline. The approver reviews what will
   224	  be applied, and is a named human who is not the author.
   225	- Higher risk adds a second, specialised approver, not a different path.
   226	- An incident uses the same path with faster people, not fewer checks.
   227	
   228	**Levels**
   229	
   230	| Level | What | Examples | Approval | Path |
   231	|---|---|---|---|---|
   232	| **L0** | Read: anything in non-prod; metadata in prod | `aws lambda get-function-configuration`, CloudWatch metrics, redacted logs, `pulumi preview` on non-prod | None | Direct, read-only identity |
   233	| **L1** | Change in non-prod (synthetic data only, GĐ-14) | Deploy a branch to dev, try a new queue setting | None beyond the author | Direct with non-prod roles, or a PR preview pipeline |
   234	| **L2** | Routine prod change, expressible as code | Lambda timeout/memory, ECS desired count, app release | 1 approval: code owner ≠ author, ≠ last pusher | PR → merge to `main` → deploy workflow |
   235	| **L3** | High-risk prod change | IAM or SCP; replace/delete of a stateful resource; network; `.github/**`, CODEOWNERS, `.claude/**`, `CLAUDE.md`, `.mcp.json`, plugin catalogue; a policy-pack violation; a real-data read (layer 6); the JIT fallback (layer 7) | L2 + platform approver | Same PR path; second approval by path rule or by the deploy job's risk stop |
   236	
   237	**On Papaya's stack**
   238	
   239	- **The gate is the merge to `main`.** On GitHub Team, environment required reviewers are not
   240	  available for private repos (📖 R3.1.2; Team is the owner's assumption GĐ-5). A ruleset on `main`
   241	  sets (📖 R3.2.3, R3.2.5):
   242	  - require a pull request, 1 approval, code-owner review;
   243	  - dismiss stale approvals when the diff changes;
   244	  - require approval from someone other than the last person to push;
   245	  - required status checks with a pinned source app;
   246	  - block force pushes; empty bypass list (📖 R3.2.2).
   247	  GitHub already stops authors from approving their own PR (📖 R3.3.1).
   248	- **L3 by path:** path-based required reviewers, e.g. 1 approval from `platform` on `infra/iam/**`
   249	  and `.github/**` (📖 R3.2.4).
   250	- **L3 by preview [design]:** the deploy job on `main` runs the prod preview. If it shows
   251	  replace/delete of a stateful resource, an IAM change or a policy violation, it stops. The engineer
   252	  opens a second PR adding `deploy-approvals/<sha>.yaml` with the preview hash; platform approves;
   253	  the job re-runs and continues only if the hash matches (Options §1; hash stability ⚠️ Options §8).
   254	- **Making "merge approval = deploy approval" true** (📖 R3.6 #1):
   255	  - the `production` environment allows only `main` (📖 R3.1.3);
   256	  - only the CODEOWNERS-protected reusable deploy workflow references `production`;
   257	  - deploy workflows take no ref or SHA input and use no `issue_comment`, `pull_request_target` or
   258	    `workflow_run` trigger (📖 R3.4.5c);
   259	  - one concurrency group per stack, without `cancel-in-progress` (📖 R4.1.4).
   260	- **Pulumi:**
   261	  - PRs preview **non-prod stacks only**, because a prod preview runs PR code with state-write and
   262	    secret access (📖 R4.6 #2, R4.3.3);
   263	  - on `main`: `pulumi preview` on prod, then `pulumi up` with the policy pack, which stops on a
   264	    mandatory violation (📖 R4.2.2);
   265	  - prod runs use an allowlist of commands: no `--target`, `--replace`, `--skip-preview`, no
   266	    `state`, `import`, `destroy` (📖 R4.6 #3).
   267	- **Serverless Framework (v4):**
   268	  - `serverless package`, then `serverless diff --json` (sf-core ≥ 4.37.0). It always exits 0, so
   269	    CI parses the JSON (📖 R4.4.2);
   270	  - `serverless deploy --package` deploys the built artifact (📖 R4.4.3);
   271	  - `provider.iam.deploymentRole` set, least privilege; it cannot be removed later (📖 R4.4.5).
   272	- **Incident lane:** PR label `incident`; on-call approver paged; security checks always run (S10);
   273	  only slow, unrelated tests are skipped [design]. Path filters on required checks may leave them
   274	  pending (⚠️ Options §8).
   275	
   276	**Example: Lambda timeouts at 2 a.m.** (Options §6)
   277	
   278	1. The engineer's incident-analysis session (`--restricted`, read-only identity) reads metrics and
   279	   redacted logs, and proposes: `timeout` 30 → 90 s, `memorySize` 1024 → 2048, plus the queue's
   280	   visibility timeout.
   281	2. A second session, with no prod identity, edits `serverless.yml` and runs `serverless diff` on
   282	   non-prod. It opens a PR labelled `incident` under the engineer's GitHub identity.
   283	3. The on-call approver reviews the diff and approves; the engineer merges.
   284	4. The deploy workflow packages, previews prod, sees no risky change, deploys with the OIDC role.
   285	5. Estimated 11–46 min (⚠️ unmeasured, Options §6). The target is ≤ 30 min, so measuring this lane
   286	   is the first rollout task (layer 8).
   287	
   288	**Audit evidence**
   289	
   290	- The PR: diff, approvers, check results, labels.
   291	- The run: prod preview artifact and deploy log, exported because run retention is limited (⚠️
   292	  duration unknown, Options §8).
   293	- CloudTrail events of the deploy role.
   294	- All linked by commit SHA and run ID, kept ≥ 6 years (📖 R5.7 #2).
   295	
   296	**Cost / value.** One approval for routine prod changes, two for risky ones; no approval in
   297	non-prod. Compliance gets four-eyes enforced by GitHub, and a record per change. The open gap: on
   298	Team, the reviewer sees the non-prod preview, not the prod diff.

   302	## Layer 4 — Supply chain for MCP servers and plugins
   303	
   304	**Principle**
   305	
   306	- An MCP server or plugin is software that runs with the engineer's access (📖 R1.6.2, R5.5.5).
   307	  It is installed only from a reviewed catalogue, pinned to a version.
   308	- The catalogue is the inventory (P4c).
   309	- Review must be fast (S6: ≤ 5 working days), or people will work around it.
   310	
   311	**On Papaya's stack**
   312	
   313	- **Plugins:**
   314	  - Papaya's private marketplace is a git repo with `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` (📖 R1.4.1);
   315	    sources pinned to a `ref` or `sha` (📖 R1.4.1b, link only).
   316	  - Managed: `strictKnownMarketplaces` = Papaya's marketplace only; `blockedMarketplaces`;
   317	    `enabledPlugins: false` to pull one plugin (📖 R1.4.2). The allowlist is checked before download
   318	    and at every session start (📖 R1.4.3).
   319	  - Client ≥ 2.1.179, which fixes a plugin-pin bypass (📖 R6.2.7).
   320	  - Plugin hooks run only if the plugin is force-enabled in managed `enabledPlugins` (📖 R1.5.4).
   321	    User-installed mods are blocked with `allowManagedModsOnly` (📖 R1.5.6).
   322	- **MCP servers:**
   323	  - "Approved catalogue" pattern: `allowedMcpServers` + `allowManagedMcpServersOnly: true`
   324	    (📖 R1.3.1, R1.3.4). Without the lock, users can widen the list (📖 R1.3.4).
   325	  - Match by `serverUrl` or `serverCommand`, never `serverName` ("not a security control",
   326	    📖 R1.3.3). The `serverCommand` argv pins an exact version, not `@latest` (⚠️ inference, R6.4.4).
   327	  - `disableClaudeAiConnectors: true` (📖 R1.3.7).
   328	  - With MDM, `managed-mcp.json` gives exclusive control; it cannot be delivered server-managed
   329	    (📖 R1.3.2).
   330	- **Review checklist (one page per entry)** [design]:
   331	  - publisher and licence; local (`stdio`) or remote (📖 R6.4.3);
   332	  - what it can read and where it sends data; an entry in the supplier register with BAA/DPA status
   333	    (📖 R5.6);
   334	  - authentication: no token passthrough (📖 R6.4.2);
   335	  - write tools (Slack, Linear, GitHub) enabled only where needed (📖 R6.5 #6);
   336	  - pinned version; reviewer's name; review date.
   337	- **Known gaps:** server-managed settings cannot target groups (📖 R1.1.6, R1.4.4); changes to a
   338	  server's tool descriptions after approval are not covered (⚠️ R6.6).
   339	
   340	**Audit evidence**
   341	
   342	- The catalogue file in git: each addition is a PR with an approver.
   343	- The supplier register.
   344	- OTel `claude_code.plugin_installed` / `plugin_loaded` (📖 R1.4.6) and MCP tool usage with
   345	  `OTEL_LOG_TOOL_DETAILS=1` (📖 R1.3.8).
   346	
   347	**Cost / value.** Engineers wait up to 5 days for a new tool, and request it with a short form.
   348	Compliance gets an asset inventory and supplier records for every extension.

   352	## Layer 5 — CI/CD hardening
   353	
   354	**Principle**
   355	
   356	- CI is the only production writer, so it is the most valuable target.
   357	- No long-lived credentials; least-privilege, short-lived tokens per job.
   358	- Every third-party dependency of CI is pinned.
   359	- Code written by an agent goes through the same checks as code written by a person (S10).
   360	
   361	**On Papaya's stack**
   362	
   363	- **Credentials:**
   364	  - replace AWS keys and long-lived tokens in repository secrets with OIDC (📖 R3.4.1). Anyone with
   365	    write access can read repository secrets (📖 R3.5.3b);
   366	  - `GITHUB_TOKEN` default "restricted"; each job declares `permissions:` (📖 R3.5.3);
   367	  - "Allow GitHub Actions to create and approve pull requests" stays **off** (📖 R3.3.3);
   368	  - Serverless License Key in SSM per account, read by the deploy role (sf ≥ 4.4.19, 📖 R4.4.8).
   369	- **Close side paths to prod:** do not use Pulumi ESC, Pulumi Deployments or Serverless Dashboard
   370	  providers for prod. If used, they get the same trust and audit as the GitHub path (📖 R4.4.9, R4.6 #6).
   371	- **Pin CI dependencies:** workflow lockfile (`gh actions-lock`) or full SHA, enforced by the org
   372	  policy "Require actions to be pinned to a full-length commit SHA" (📖 R3.5.5).
   373	- **Protect the files that control CI and agents:** CODEOWNERS on `.github/`, `CLAUDE.md`,
   374	  `.claude/`, `.mcp.json`, `.husky/`, `.gitmodules` (📖 R3.2.8, R6.5 #5). CI validates the CODEOWNERS
   375	  file, because invalid lines are skipped silently (📖 R3.2.7).
   376	- **No privileged triggers with untrusted code:** no `pull_request_target` or `workflow_run` job
   377	  that checks out PR code (📖 R3.5.4).
   378	- **Agents in CI:**
   379	  - `claude-code-action` with a custom GitHub App limited to Contents, Issues and Pull requests
   380	    (📖 R1.7.8);
   381	  - authentication through Anthropic workload identity federation, not a personal OAuth token
   382	    (📖 R1.7.6);
   383	  - `--restricted`, explicit `--tools`, repo hooks disabled (D3, 📖 R1.7.3);
   384	  - never `environment: production` (📖 R3.6 #4);
   385	  - a managed-settings file written on a GitHub-hosted runner is advisory, because the job has
   386	    `sudo` (📖 R3.5.1);
   387	  - an app-authored PR needs 2 approvals, or a pinned check confirming a human approver who did not
   388	    trigger the run (📖 R3.3.4, R3.7 A3).
   389	- **Required security checks on every PR to `main` (S10):** secret scanning, dependency check, SAST.
   390	  The products are **not researched** (⚠️ gap G4). Two checks are researched: IAM Access Analyzer
   391	  `check-no-new-access` (📖 R2.8.1) and Pulumi policy packs (📖 R4.2.2).
   392	
   393	**Audit evidence**
   394	
   395	- Workflow files and the lockfile in git.
   396	- Org audit log events: `org.update_actions_secret`, `repo.set_workflow_permission_can_approve_pr`,
   397	  `repository_ruleset.update` (📖 R3.3.3, R3.5.6).
   398	- Check results attached to each PR.
   399	- Inventory of repository secrets: target zero cloud credentials (S2).
   400	
   401	**Cost / value.** One-time migration of workflows to OIDC and pinned actions; slower updates of
   402	actions. Compliance gets S2 (no long-lived CI credentials) and the same guardrails for agent and
   403	human code.

   407	## Layer 6 — Data protection and prompt-injection defence
   408	
   409	**Principle**
   410	
   411	- By default the coding agent never sees real customer data (PHI and other sensitive data). This
   412	  is enforced by what its identity can read, not by instructions (owner decision, R1.9).
   413	- Reading real data is an exception: requested, approved, scoped to one tenant, time-boxed, done by
   414	  a human (GĐ-9).
   415	- Everything the agent reads from outside is untrusted: logs, tickets, PR text, web pages, MCP
   416	  results, uploaded documents.
   417	- Untrusted reading and write power are kept apart.
   418	- Non-prod holds synthetic data only (GĐ-14).
   419	
   420	**On Papaya's stack**
   421	
   422	- **Close the paths by identity.** `work/02-research.md` 1.11.4 lists 17 ways PHI can reach the
   423	  agent (logs, SQL, S3, queues, tickets, uploaded documents, exec, replay…). The main control is that
   424	  agent-usable identities have **no data-plane read** on PHI stores (R1.11.4, R2.9 #4):
   425	  - SCP deny on claim buckets and queues (layer 1);
   426	  - no Aurora credentials for engineers' agents; no `ecs:ExecuteCommand`, `kubectl exec` or
   427	    `lambda:InvokeFunction` in prod for agent-usable roles [design];
   428	  - logs redacted at source, raw payloads in a separate log group that `ProdReadMetadata` cannot read
   429	    [design];
   430	  - alerts carry IDs and links, not payloads [design].
   431	- **The real-data exception (GĐ-9)** [design]:
   432	  - a request names the tenant, the purpose, the store and the time window; the data owner and an
   433	    approver ≠ requester approve it (L3);
   434	  - a JIT data-read role, tenant-scoped through session tags (ABAC, 📖 R2.1.8), TTL 1 h;
   435	  - the human reads in a terminal without an agent; the agent only gets a redacted summary.
   436	  - Reason: the Team plan is not covered by Anthropic's BAA (⚠️ R1.11.3.4), and the owner keeps
   437	    the agent outside PHI scope. Enterprise + BAA only for a separate environment if a real need
   438	    appears (R1.9).
   439	- **Partner-uploaded claim documents (A7b).** Never open a raw partner document in an agent
   440	  session. Reproduce bugs with synthetic documents; if a real one is needed, a de-identification
   441	  step comes first [design]. The document is both PHI and an injection vector (R1.11.4 L6).
   442	- **Injection controls** (each lowers frequency; layers 1 and 3 limit impact):
   443	  - Manual mode by default (layer 2); the auto-mode classifier misses some actions (17 % of 52 real
   444	    overeager actions, 📖 R6.3.5);
   445	  - narrow sandbox egress; a broad domain such as `github.com` is a documented exfiltration path
   446	    (📖 R6.3.7);
   447	  - incident analysis in `--restricted` with no edit tools (layer 2), so a poisoned log line can at
   448	    most produce a bad *proposal* that a human reviews in a PR;
   449	  - shared knowledge in `CLAUDE.md` under CODEOWNERS; personal auto memory is reviewed by the
   450	    engineer and is a residual risk with a named owner (D3, 📖 R6.3.11).
   451	- **Tenant isolation:** one tenant per data-read grant (ABAC tags); a session that read tenant X's
   452	  data writes nothing to shared places (PRs, tickets, chat) without review (⚠️ process; R6.5 #8).
   453	  Tenants are assumed to be separated logically in shared accounts (GĐ-3, unconfirmed).
   454	- **Non-prod:** no copies from prod to non-prod (GĐ-14, S11); periodic scan of non-prod stores
   455	  (tool not researched, ⚠️ gap G4).
   456	- **Local stores:** session transcripts and memory hold what the agent read (R1.11.4). Set
   457	  `cleanupPeriodDays` (the HIPAA sample uses 30, 📖 R5.5.4).
   458	- **Cross-border (Vietnam example):** using a model provider outside Vietnam on Vietnamese personal
   459	  data is a transfer; agent data flows update Papaya's existing transfer impact assessment (📖
   460	  R5.4.5; application ⚠️ R5.7 #4, needs Papaya legal).
   461	
   462	**Audit evidence**
   463	
   464	- SCP and IAM text (the written access rule for sensitive data, 📖 R5.4.3 Art. 4(2), R5.7 #5).
   465	- Data-read requests: requester, approver, tenant, window; CloudTrail of the data-read role.
   466	- Planted-injection test reports (layer 8).
   467	- Non-prod scan results (S11).
   468	
   469	**Cost / value.** Engineers debug with redacted data and synthetic documents; the rare real-data
   470	read needs a request. Compliance gets "minimum necessary" (📖 R1.11.1.8) enforced by IAM, and a
   471	record for every exception (S9).

   475	## Layer 7 — Observability, audit, incident response and break-glass
   476	
   477	**Principle**
   478	
   479	- One evidence chain per production change: who proposed, who approved, what was approved, what was
   480	  applied, when. Tamper-evident, retained ≥ 6 years.
   481	- Every prod alert goes to one routed channel with a named on-call, including on-call approvers.
   482	- Unapproved change is detected: drift and reconciliation are compliance signals.
   483	- Break-glass is time-boxed, approved before use, and reconciled after.
   484	
   485	**On Papaya's stack**
   486	
   487	- **Audit stores:**
   488	  - an organization CloudTrail to a log-archive account, integrity validation on, retention chosen
   489	    on purpose (📖 R2.6.2, R2.6.4, R2.6.5). Data events only on PHI buckets, prod Lambda invoke and
   490	    prod SQS, because of cost (📖 R2.6.3). CloudTrail Lake is closed to new customers (📖 R2.6.6);
   491	  - the GitHub org audit log keeps 180 days, so it is exported (📖 R3.5.6, R3.6 #6);
   492	  - alerts on `repository_ruleset.update`, `environment.update_protection_rule`,
   493	    `environment.delete`, `protected_branch.policy_override` (📖 R3.6 #1);
   494	  - Aurora: SQL is not in CloudTrail; Database Activity Streams, with an alert on lost-record events,
   495	    and the stream treated as PHI (📖 R2.7.1, R2.7.2; Serverless v2 support ⚠️);
   496	  - Claude Code OTel without prompt content (layer 2).
   497	- **Change record [design]:** one record per prod change, keyed by commit SHA or JIT request ID,
   498	  linking the PR, approvals, run ID, preview artifact and CloudTrail events. Monthly reconciliation:
   499	  every prod write event in CloudTrail must match an approved record (S3). The actor is
   500	  `onBehalfOf.userId` where present, otherwise the session name plus a directory lookup (📖 R2.5.1,
   501	  R2.5.3; ⚠️ on member-account events).
   502	- **Drift detection:** scheduled `pulumi preview --refresh --expect-no-changes` or
   503	  `pulumi refresh --preview-only` (📖 R4.3.1, R4.3.2); CloudFormation drift detection for Serverless
   504	  stacks, which checks only explicitly set properties (📖 R4.4.6). Auto-remediation stays off, since it
   505	  is an unreviewed `up` (📖 R4.3.2). Each finding maps to a CloudTrail actor and a ticket.
   506	- **On-call (phase 1 minimum):** a named schedule with primary and backup approvers, 24/7, and one
   507	  alert route for prod. The paging and alert tools are not researched (⚠️ gap G5).
   508	- **Break-glass = the JIT fallback (D2'):**
   509	
   510	  | Step | Rule |
   511	  |---|---|
   512	  | When | Only if the PR path cannot be used: the operation is not in IaC and has no runbook, or GitHub/Actions is down, or the pipeline is broken. The approver refuses JIT for anything expressible in IaC while the pipeline works, and records why. |
   513	  | Request | Exact command list, service scope, reason, incident link. |
   514	  | Approval | On-call approver ≠ requester, before the grant. |
   515	  | Grant | `InfraOperator-<service>`, session 1 h (📖 R2.1.4). Removing the assignment stops only new sessions (📖 R2.4.3); an immediate stop needs the revocation SCP (📖 R2.4.4). |
   516	  | Execution | The human types the commands in a terminal with no agent. The agent may propose them from its read-only session (D2). |
   517	  | After | A reconciliation job compares CloudTrail writes in the window with the list [design]. A codify PR within a fixed deadline; drift detection until it merges. |
   518	  | Identity Center outage | Emergency direct SAML federation to roles, built and tested in advance, with its own approval and alerting (📖 R2.1.11, R2.9 #2). |
   519	
   520	  Metrics with review thresholds: JIT grants per month; JIT used for changes that should have gone
   521	  through a PR; windows with writes outside the list; codify PRs past deadline (Options §7). When a
   522	  threshold is passed, the fix is to make the PR lane faster or move the operation to a runbook,
   523	  not to widen JIT.
   524	- **Later (roadmap):** pre-approved runbooks for repeated non-IaC operations, if SSM Automation's
   525	  approval step meets three conditions (Options §2, §7; this source is outside `02-research.md`,
   526	  see G6); break-glass from an environment without the agent installed (D2, ⚠️).
   527	
   528	**Audit evidence**
   529	
   530	- CloudTrail with digest validation; exported GitHub audit log.
   531	- Change records and the monthly reconciliation report (S3, S8: evidence for a random change in
   532	  ≤ 30 min).
   533	- JIT request records, reconciliation results, codify PRs.
   534	- Drift reports and their tickets.
   535	- On-call schedule and approval-wait times (S7).
   536	
   537	**Cost / value.** The incident lane is slower than direct access (estimated 11–46 min vs 2–20,
   538	Options §6), and engineers must write a command list for JIT. Compliance gets approval before apply
   539	even in incidents, and detection when something slips through.

   543	## Layer 8 — Governance and rollout
   544	
   545	**Principle**
   546	
   547	- Every control has an owner, a metric and a review date.
   548	- Policies live in git and change through PRs, like code.
   549	- Access is never removed before its replacement works (otherwise people break the rule in the
   550	  first incident).
   551	- Controls are tested against real attacks, not only reviewed on paper.
   552	
   553	**On Papaya's stack**
   554	
   555	- **Ownership:**
   556	
   557	  | Area | Owner |
   558	  |---|---|
   559	  | Framework, managed settings, MCP/plugin catalogue, metrics | DevSecOps |
   560	  | Deploy workflows, rulesets, Pulumi/Serverless pipelines | Platform team |
   561	  | Evidence review, supplier register, ISO Statement of Applicability | Compliance |
   562	  | CODEOWNERS for each domain | Engineering leads |
   563	  | On-call schedule (incl. approvers) | Engineering management |
   564	
   565	- **Policy as code:** managed settings, the MCP/plugin catalogue, SCPs, trust policies, CODEOWNERS
   566	  and deploy workflows are files in the monorepo under CODEOWNERS. GitHub settings that are not files
   567	  (rulesets, environment config) are compared with an expected copy by a scheduled job (📖 R3.6 #1;
   568	  endpoint ⚠️).
   569	- **Metrics** (from framing §5): S1 standing prod-write = 0; S2 cloud credentials in GitHub
   570	  secrets = 0; S3 unmatched prod changes = 0; S4 approval wait ≤ 1 h; S5 machines with the policy =
   571	  100 %; S6 tools outside the catalogue = 0; S7 incident approval wait ≤ 15 min; S8 evidence in
   572	  ≤ 30 min; S9 data reads without approval = 0; S10 PRs with checks = 100 %; S11 prod copies in
   573	  non-prod = 0.
   574	- **Rollout** (≈ 90 days, GĐ-12 unconfirmed). Order from framing round 1 Q8, with the on-call
   575	  approver list moved into phase 1:
   576	
   577	  | Phase | Days | Items |
   578	  |---|---|---|
   579	  | 1 | 0–30 | OIDC for all deploy workflows, remove AWS keys and long-lived tokens; rulesets and CODEOWNERS on `main`; on-call approver schedule (primary + backup); managed settings baseline; MCP/plugin catalogue v1; **measure the incident PR lane on one real service** (target ≤ 30 min). |
   580	  | 2 | 30–60 | Remove standing prod write (only after the approver schedule and JIT fallback work); SCP data-plane deny; drift detection; audit-log export and change records; Aurora activity streams. |
   581	  | 3 | 60–90 | Planted-injection test on the real configuration (📖 R6.1.1, R6.1.5, R6.5 #12); auto-mode non-prod environment (D1 c); verify the SSM runbook option; first evidence pack for ISO 27001; 90-day review of D1–D3. |
   582	
   583	- **Exceptions:** any deviation from this framework is a ticket with an owner, a reason and an
   584	  expiry date.
   585	- **Mapping to standards:** HIPAA Security Rule sections are cited from the text (📖 R5.2); ISO/IEC
   586	  27001:2022 Annex A control IDs are recalled, not read, and must be checked against a licensed copy
   587	  (⚠️ R5.1). The "named human approves" rule is Papaya's chosen way to meet change-management and
   588	  segregation-of-duties controls, not a literal requirement (⚠️ R5.7 #1).
   589	- **Out of scope:** security of Papaya's production AI agents (A7a). Noted as a follow-up.
   590	
   591	**Audit evidence**
   592	
   593	- Git history of every policy file.
   594	- Monthly metric report; 90-day review minutes; exception register.
   595	- Planted-injection test reports.
   596	
   597	**Cost / value.** A small, steady overhead for DevSecOps and platform. Compliance gets a managed
   598	system with owners and review dates, which ISO 27001 auditors look for.
