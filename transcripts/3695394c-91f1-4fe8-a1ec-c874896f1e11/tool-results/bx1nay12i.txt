     1	# Framework: coding agents for DevSecOps at Papaya
     2	
     3	> Status: draft v2, for owner review · Updated: 2026-11-09
     4	> Audience: a panel of Engineering and Compliance. Every layer states what it costs engineers and
     5	> what it gives Compliance.
     6	
     7	## How to read this
     8	
     9	The framework has eight layers. Each layer has three parts:
    10	
    11	- **Principle**: the rule, independent of any tool. It still holds if a tool changes.
    12	- **On Papaya's stack**: how the rule is applied with Pulumi, Serverless Framework, GitHub
    13	  Actions, AWS, GCP, k0s and Claude Code.
    14	- **Audit evidence**: the records the layer produces, so an auditor can test it without asking
    15	  anyone.
    16	
    17	Markers (see `CLAUDE.md` Rule 1). R*x.y* is a row in `work/02-research.md`; that row quotes and
    18	links the official page.
    19	
    20	| Marker | Meaning |
    21	|---|---|
    22	| 📖 R*x.y* | Documented behaviour: the research row is ✅ (page read) or 📖 (page linked). |
    23	| ⚠️ R*x.y* | The research row is an inference, an open question, or a "What this means for Papaya" conclusion (sections 1.8, 2.9, 3.6, 3.7, 4.6, 5.6, 5.7, 6.5). Reasoned, not documented. |
    24	| [design] | A mechanism I propose, built from documented parts but not tested. Treat it as ⚠️. |
    25	| ✅ | Checked locally in this repository. |
    26	
    27	Other references: **GĐ-n** = owner-confirmed assumption n in `work/01-problem-framing.md` §4;
    28	**S-n** = success criterion n (same file, §5); **P-n / A-n** = problems (same file, §1);
    29	**D1–D3** = owner decisions in `work/02-research.md` 6.6; **Options §n** = `work/03-options.md`;
    30	**G-n** = gaps at the end of this file.

    34	## 1. The decision in one page
    35	
    36	**The conflict is narrow.** Engineering and Compliance disagree only about *writes to
    37	production*. Reading metadata and everything in non-prod are outside Compliance's sentence
    38	(framing §3.1). Reading real customer data is a separate question (layer 6).
    39	
    40	**Chosen approach: "A + D2'"** (Options §9, owner decision 2026-11-09):
    41	
    42	1. **Every production change is a pull request**, approved by a named human who is not the author,
    43	   and applied by the CI pipeline. This holds during incidents too ("IaC first").
    44	2. **A narrow fallback (break-glass)**: only when the PR path cannot be used, a human gets a
    45	   short, approved just-in-time (JIT) permission and runs an approved command list in a terminal
    46	   without an agent [design]. Details in layer 7.
    47	3. **Nobody holds standing production write access**, and the agent is never given a production
    48	   write identity.
    49	
    50	| Engineering gets | Compliance gets |
    51	|---|---|
    52	| A free loop in non-prod: the agent reads, changes and deploys without approval (safe because non-prod holds synthetic data only, GĐ-14). | Every prod change approved **before** apply by a named human ≠ author (GĐ-8, GĐ-11), enforced by GitHub rulesets and AWS IAM rather than by the agent. |
    53	| Read-only prod metadata for the agent (config, metrics, redacted logs). | No standing prod write for any human (S1); no long-lived keys in CI (S2). |
    54	| Self-service: open a PR, get one approval, ship the same day (S4: wait ≤ 1 h in working hours). | One evidence chain per change. Approval and change records kept ≥ 6 years (📖 R5.2.9). |
    55	| An incident lane: approval wait ≤ 15 min 24/7 (S7), decision-to-apply ≤ 30 min (Options §9 Q3). | Agent-side controls (managed settings, tool catalogue) as a second layer, not as the control of record. |
    56	
    57	**Where the design does not fully meet Compliance's sentence (stated openly):**
    58	
    59	- **The JIT fallback meets it only partly.** The approver approves a command list, not an applied
    60	  change, and "the human, not the agent, ran it" is a process rule (Options §5.1). The owner
    61	  accepted this (Options §9 Q2). It is measured (layer 7).
    62	- **On the GitHub Team plan nobody sees the prod diff before apply** (⚠️ R4.6 #1; Team is the
    63	  owner's assumption GĐ-5). The reviewer sees the code diff and a non-prod preview. The deploy job
    64	  stops on risky prod diffs and asks for a second approval [design].
    65	- **Admins can edit the gate.** Repository admins and holders of "edit repository rules" can
    66	  change or disable rulesets; this is logged, not prevented (📖 R3.2.2, ⚠️ R3.6 #1). The agent on
    67	  a laptop acts with its engineer's GitHub login, so an admin's agent has the same power. Few
    68	  admins, access reviews and alerts mitigate this (layers 7, 8).
    69	- **Incident approvers are scarce.** If the on-call approver is a code owner of every path, domain
    70	  review gets weaker; L3 incidents need a platform approver on call 24/7 with a backup, which a
    71	  1–2 person DevSecOps team cannot staff alone (Options §1, §7; GĐ-1).
    72	- **HIPAA applicability is a legal question** (⚠️ R1.11.1.11). The design follows the brief and
    73	  treats Papaya as a business associate.
    74	
    75	**Cross-cutting rules** (from the owner's balance principle, `work/02-research.md` 6.8):
    76	
    77	1. **The boundary is IAM and the CI gate.** Agent-side controls lower how often things go wrong;
    78	   the docs call managed settings "a client-side control, not a security boundary" (📖 R1.1.7).
    79	2. **Assume prompt injection succeeds.** No source claims a full prevention (📖 R6.1.1, R6.1.2).
    80	3. **Friction where damage is large, none where it is small.** If the safe path is slower than the
    81	   shortcut, people take the shortcut (P3b). So friction is measured and has a time-boxed escape.
    82	4. **Nothing standing.** No standing prod write, no long-lived credentials.
    83	5. **Evidence by construction.** The path itself produces the records.
    84	
    85	### Roles
    86	
    87	| Role | Who | Rule |
    88	|---|---|---|
    89	| Author | The engineer, and the agent working for them | Never approves their own change. GitHub blocks PR authors from approving (📖 R3.3.1). |
    90	| Approver | A named human with write access, listed in CODEOWNERS | ≠ author, ≠ last pusher (📖 R3.2.3). For a PR opened by a GitHub App, ≠ the person who triggered it (⚠️ R3.3.4, enforced by a check, layer 5). |
    91	| On-call approver | Primary + backup, 24/7, named in the on-call schedule | Same rule as Approver. Target: approval wait ≤ 15 min (S7). |
    92	| Platform approver | Platform team member, also on the on-call schedule | Second approval for L3 changes. |
    93	| Control owner | DevSecOps (1–2 people, GĐ-1) | Owns policy files and metrics. Is **not** the only approver. |
    94	
    95	"An agent never approves" (GĐ-11) is enforced only partly: GitHub blocks self-approval, but an
    96	agent using its engineer's `gh` login could approve **someone else's** PR as that engineer. The
    97	auto-mode classifier blocks only "approving Claude's own pull request" (📖 R6.3.3). This is a
    98	process rule with residual risk; detection is in layer 7 [design].

   102	## Layer 1 — Identity and access
   103	
   104	**Principle**
   105	
   106	- Every action traces to one named human.
   107	- Nobody holds standing write access to production. Production is written by the pipeline role,
   108	  or, in the fallback, by an approved, time-boxed human grant.
   109	- Production reads default to metadata. Reads of stores that hold customer data are blocked by a
   110	  guardrail that member-account identities cannot edit.
   111	- No long-lived credentials. Prod and non-prod are separate boundaries.
   112	
   113	**On Papaya's stack**
   114	
   115	- **The human's access is the agent's access.** On a laptop the agent uses the engineer's AWS SSO
   116	  cache, and one portal session reaches every role assigned to the engineer (📖 R2.2.1, R2.2.2). A
   117	  separate "agent" permission set therefore does not separate them (⚠️ R2.9 #1). The control is
   118	  what the **engineer** holds.
   119	- **AWS IAM Identity Center permission sets** (📖 R2.1.1):
   120	  - `ProdReadMetadata`: config, metrics and redacted log groups in prod; session duration 1 h, the
   121	    minimum (📖 R2.1.4). Read actions that return secrets in configuration (for example Lambda
   122	    environment variables) are excluded, because resolved values may sit there in plain text
   123	    (⚠️ R4.4.7); secrets are resolved at runtime instead (⚠️ R4.6 #7).
   124	  - `NonProdDeveloper`: broad rights in non-prod accounts only.
   125	  - **No standing prod-write permission set.**
   126	  - `InfraOperator-<service>`: assigned only by the JIT flow (layer 7). Tooling: a partner product,
   127	    the TEAM sample, or a small flow on `CreateAccountAssignment` / `DeleteAccountAssignment`
   128	    (📖 R2.4.1, R2.4.1b, R2.4.2).
   129	- **Guardrails in the prod OU (SCPs)**, which cap member-account principals (📖 R2.3.1, R2.3.2) [design]:
   130	  - deny data-plane reads on stores holding claim data (e.g. `s3:GetObject` on the claim-documents
   131	    bucket, `sqs:ReceiveMessage`, `states:GetExecutionHistory`) except application roles and the
   132	    approved data-read role, selected by `aws:PrincipalArn` (📖 R2.3.8; pattern ⚠️ R2.9 #4);
   133	  - deny `lambda:UpdateFunctionCode` and `lambda:UpdateFunctionConfiguration` except the
   134	    CloudFormation `deploymentRole` and the named JIT roles, to close `serverless deploy function`,
   135	    which changes config outside CloudFormation (📖 R4.4.4; pattern ⚠️ R4.6 #3; SCP or IAM not chosen);
   136	  - a pre-deployed revocation Deny on `identitystore:userId` (📖 R2.4.4);
   137	  - protect the organization trail and log archive (📖 R2.6.2).
   138	  Limits: 10 SCPs per target, 10,240 characters each; nothing applies to the management account
   139	  (📖 R2.3.2, R2.3.4).
   140	- **Sessions and MFA:** set the access-portal session length on purpose; the IdP session can
   141	  silently extend it (📖 R2.1.5, R2.1.6). MFA is managed in the external IdP if one is used (📖 R2.1.7).
   142	- **CI → AWS:** GitHub OIDC, no stored keys (📖 R3.4.1). The prod deploy role trusts only tokens
   143	  from the `production` environment of Papaya's monorepo, in the repo's `sub` format (📖 R3.4.3,
   144	  R3.4.4; the immutable format is assumed, ⚠️ R3.7 A2). Preferably the `sub` is customised to also
   145	  include the reusable deploy workflow's `job_workflow_ref` (📖 R3.4.5; availability on Team ⚠️, G10).
   146	  Non-prod roles trust non-prod environments or `pull_request` (⚠️ R4.6 #2).
   147	- **CI → GCP:** Workload Identity Federation with an attribute condition on Papaya's GitHub
   148	  organization, which Google requires (📖 R3.4.5b).
   149	- **GCP, k0s and Firebase for humans:** same principles; mechanisms not researched (G1–G3). Today
   150	  engineers reach k0s with kubeconfigs and no SSO (GĐ-4, unconfirmed). Interim rule: no admin
   151	  kubeconfig and no GCP owner role on laptops; prod deploys to k0s and Firebase run only from CI.
   152	
   153	**Audit evidence**
   154	
   155	- Export of Identity Center assignments: no standing prod-write assignment (S1).
   156	- CloudTrail `CreateAccountAssignment` / `DeleteAccountAssignment` per JIT grant (📖 R2.4.2), linked
   157	  to the approval record by request ID [design].
   158	- SCPs and trust policies as Pulumi code: version history = change history.
   159	- IAM Access Analyzer `check-no-new-access` on every PR that changes a policy (📖 R2.8.1).
   160	- Quarterly access review (permission sets, GitHub admins).
   161	
   162	**Cost / value.** Engineers lose standing prod write; they keep fast prod reads and a free non-prod
   163	loop. Compliance gets least privilege that is enforced, not promised.

   167	## Layer 2 — Agent runtime controls
   168	
   169	**Principle**
   170	
   171	- Agent-side controls are hygiene: they cut mistakes; they are not the boundary (📖 R1.1.7, R1.1.8).
   172	- They are set centrally, the same for everyone, and versioned in git.
   173	- Safe defaults with a measured escape hatch. A default that people keep overriding is replaced,
   174	  not defended.
   175	- The session that reads untrusted content should hold as little power as possible (📖 R6.1.2).
   176	
   177	**On Papaya's stack**
   178	
   179	- **Delivery:** server-managed settings from the claude.ai admin console (available on Team,
   180	  📖 R1.1.5) for all laptops. Where MDM exists, also an endpoint `managed-settings.json`, protected
   181	  at OS level, though a local admin can still edit it (📖 R1.1.3, R1.1.8). MDM is unknown, so both
   182	  paths are prepared (R1.9).
   183	- **Baseline keys** (template: Anthropic's `settings-hipaa.json`, 📖 R5.5.4):
   184	
   185	  | Key | Value | Why | Source |
   186	  |---|---|---|---|
   187	  | `permissions.defaultMode` | `default` (Manual) | D1. A default, not a lock: users can switch to auto; VS Code remembers the last pick. | 📖 R6.9.3 |
   188	  | `permissions.allow` (managed) | read-only commands Papaya uses, e.g. `Bash(aws cloudwatch get-metric-data *)` | Fewer prompts for safe reads (P3b) | 📖 R6.9.3 |
   189	  | `allowManagedPermissionRulesOnly` | `true` | One rule set for everyone (D1, owner Q4) | 📖 R1.2.5 |
   190	  | `permissions.disableBypassPermissionsMode` | `"disable"` | Removes "prompts off" (P3a) | 📖 R1.2.2 |
   191	  | `requiredMinimumVersion` | ≥ 2.1.295, and ≥ the latest fixed advisory at rollout | `onFailure: "block"` needs 2.1.295 (R1.5.2), `allowedProviders` 2.1.285 (R1.1.15), fail-closed locks 2.1.282 (R1.1.11); advisory history (R6.3.9) | 📖 R1.1.13 |
   192	  | `allowedProviders` + `forceLoginOrgUUID` | `["anthropic"]` + Papaya's org | On endpoint-managed machines, closes most bypasses | 📖 R1.1.15 |
   193	  | `allowManagedHooksOnly` | `true` | Repo and user hooks cannot run | 📖 R1.5.4 |
   194	  | `sandbox.enabled`, `sandbox.failIfUnavailable`, `sandbox.allowUnsandboxedCommands: false`, narrow `sandbox.network.allowedDomains` | on | Shell commands only (R1.6.2) | 📖 R1.6.4 |
   195	  | `sandbox.credentials` / `sandbox.filesystem.denyRead` on `~/.aws` | on, with `aws` in `sandbox.excludedCommands` | Trade-off below | 📖 R1.6.3, R1.6.4 |
   196	  | MCP and plugin keys | see layer 4 | | |
   197	  | OTel exporter with content flags `0` | on | Usage evidence without prompt content | 📖 R1.1.20, R5.5.4 |
   198	
   199	- **Trade-off on AWS credentials in the sandbox.** By default the sandbox can read `~/.aws`
   200	  (📖 R1.6.3). Denying it stops other shell commands from reading the credential cache, but then
   201	  the agent can call AWS only through `aws` as an excluded command, which "run[s] with full access"
   202	  (📖 R1.6.4). This is accepted because the engineer holds no prod write; the deny only narrows
   203	  which programs can take the credentials. A stronger option is a separate OS user or container for
   204	  prod-reading sessions with only `ProdReadMetadata` (⚠️ R6.5 #2, phase 3).
   205	- **Session types:**
   206	
   207	  | Session | Where | Identity | Flags | Enforcement |
   208	  |---|---|---|---|---|
   209	  | Daily development | laptop | non-prod roles + `ProdReadMetadata` | managed baseline | Auto mode allowed by choice (D1). `--restricted` recommended (D3). |
   210	  | Incident analysis | laptop | intended: `ProdReadMetadata` only | `--restricted --tools "Bash,Read,Grep,Glob"` (📖 R6.9.1b) | **Process rule** (D3, owner Q1–Q2). No Edit/Write tools, but Bash remains: writes and network calls are limited only by the sandbox and Manual prompts, and Bash can reach any role in the SSO cache (📖 R2.2.2). |
   211	  | CI agent | GitHub-hosted runner | its own GitHub App token; **no** prod environment | `--restricted`, explicit `--tools`, rules only from `--settings <runner file>` | In the workflow file under CODEOWNERS (D3, owner Q5). |
   212	  | JIT terminal (fallback only) | laptop | JIT grant | **no agent running** | **Process rule** (D2). |
   213	  | Auto-mode environment (phase 3) | separate environment, non-prod credentials only | non-prod | auto mode encouraged | D1 option (c). |
   214	
   215	- **Hard limits, stated openly:**
   216	  - Bash permission rules are not a security boundary (📖 R1.2.6).
   217	  - Hooks fail open unless `"onFailure": "block"` is set (📖 R1.5.2).
   218	  - `--restricted` cannot be forced by a setting on laptops (📖 R6.9.1, R6.9.1c).
   219	  - **On laptops without MDM there is no way to close the bypasses** of server-managed settings: a
   220	    different login, an API key or a `CLAUDE_CODE_USE_*` variable skips them, including
   221	    `allowedProviders` sent from the server (📖 R1.1.7; ⚠️ R1.8 #3; G11). The compensating controls
   222	    are IAM and the CI gate.
   223	  - In a JIT window the grant sits in the same SSO cache the agent can reach (📖 R2.2.2). "No agent
   224	    in the JIT terminal" is a process control (D2).
   225	- **Personal auto memory:** engineers review it periodically and delete it after a session that
   226	  handled suspicious content; the rest is a residual risk with a named owner (D3).
   227	- **Fallback:** switch to `disableAutoMode` if the planted-injection test (layer 8) steers auto
   228	  mode, a serious advisory appears, or an audit finds auto mode touched a protected scope (D1).
   229	
   230	**Audit evidence**
   231	
   232	- The managed settings file in git, with PR approvals.
   233	- `/status` and `claude doctor` output from sampled machines (📖 R1.1.18).
   234	- OTel: `claude_code.tool_decision` with `permission_mode`; `claude_code.tool.blocked_on_user`
   235	  with `duration_ms` as a rubber-stamping signal (D1 metrics in research 6.6).
   236	- Settings-change audit events exist; availability on Team is unknown (⚠️ R1.1.12).
   237	
   238	**Cost / value.** Manual mode prompts more than auto mode; the managed read-only allowlist offsets
   239	part of it. Compliance gets an organization policy with evidence instead of personal choices (P3c).

   243	## Layer 3 — Change workflow by risk level
   244	
   245	**Principle**
   246	
   247	- The level is set by the damage a change can do, not by who asks.
   248	- Above non-prod, one path: a reviewed PR, applied by the pipeline. The approver reviews what will
   249	  be applied and is a named human who is not the author.
   250	- Higher risk adds a specialised second approver, not a different path.
   251	- An incident uses the same path with faster people, not fewer checks.
   252	
   253	**Levels**
   254	
   255	| Level | What | Examples | Approver | Path |
   256	|---|---|---|---|---|
   257	| **L0** | Read: anything in non-prod; metadata in prod | `aws cloudwatch get-metric-data`, redacted logs, `pulumi preview` on non-prod | none | Direct, read-only identity |
   258	| **L1** | Change in non-prod (synthetic data, GĐ-14) | Deploy a branch to dev, try a queue setting | none beyond the author | Direct with non-prod roles, or PR pipeline |
   259	| **L2** | Routine prod change expressible as code | Lambda timeout/memory, ECS desired count, app release | 1 code owner ≠ author, ≠ last pusher | PR → merge to `main` → deploy workflow |
   260	| **L3** | High-risk prod change | IAM or SCP; replace/delete of a stateful resource; network; `.github/**`, CODEOWNERS, agent config files (layer 5), plugin/MCP catalogue; policy-pack violation | L2 + platform approver | Same PR path; second approval by path rule or by the deploy job's risk stop |
   261	| **L3-JIT** | Break-glass when the PR path cannot be used | ECS force-redeploy while Actions is down | on-call approver ≠ requester (Options §4b) | JIT fallback, layer 7 |
   262	| **L3-data** | Read of real customer data | Inspect one tenant's failed claim record | data owner + approver ≠ requester | Data-read request, layer 6 |
   263	
   264	**On Papaya's stack**
   265	
   266	- **The gate is the merge to `main`.** On GitHub Team, environment required reviewers are not
   267	  available for private repos (📖 R3.1.2; Team is assumed, GĐ-5). A ruleset on `main` sets
   268	  (📖 R3.2.3, R3.2.5):
   269	  - require a pull request, 1 approval, code-owner review;
   270	  - dismiss stale approvals when the diff changes;
   271	  - require approval from someone other than the last person to push;
   272	  - required status checks with a pinned source app;
   273	  - block force pushes; empty bypass list (📖 R3.2.2).
   274	- **L3 by path:** path-based required reviewers, e.g. 1 approval from `platform` on `infra/iam/**`
   275	  and `.github/**` (📖 R3.2.4).
   276	- **L3 by preview [design]:** the deploy job on `main` runs the prod preview. On replace/delete of a
   277	  stateful resource, an IAM change or a policy violation, it stops. The engineer opens a second PR
   278	  adding `deploy-approvals/<sha>.yaml` with the preview hash; platform approves; the job re-runs
   279	  and continues only if the hash matches (Options §1; hash stability ⚠️ Options §8).
   280	- **Making "merge approval = deploy approval" true** (⚠️ R3.6 #1, built on 📖 R3.1.3, R3.4.5c):
   281	  - the `production` environment allows only `main`;
   282	  - only the CODEOWNERS-protected reusable deploy workflow references `production`;
   283	  - deploy workflows take no ref or SHA input and use no `issue_comment`, `pull_request_target`
   284	    or `workflow_run` trigger;
   285	  - one concurrency group per stack, without `cancel-in-progress` (📖 R4.1.4).
   286	- **Pulumi:**
   287	  - PRs preview **non-prod stacks only**: a prod preview would run PR code with state-write and
   288	    secret access (📖 R4.3.3, R4.3.4; ⚠️ R4.6 #2);
   289	  - on `main`: `pulumi preview` on prod, then `pulumi up` with the policy pack; a mandatory
   290	    violation stops the deployment (📖 R4.2.2);
   291	  - prod runs use an allowlist: no `--target`, `--target-replace`, `--replace`, `--exclude` or
   292	    `--skip-preview`; no `state`, `import`, `destroy` or `stack rm` (⚠️ R4.6 #3; flags 📖 R4.1.2).
   293	- **Serverless Framework (v4):**
   294	  - `serverless package`, then `serverless diff --json` (sf-core ≥ 4.37.0). "Differences against the
   295	    deployed stack do not cause a non-zero exit code", so CI parses the JSON (📖 R4.4.2);
   296	  - `serverless deploy --package` deploys that artifact (📖 R4.4.3);
   297	  - a least-privilege `provider.iam.deploymentRole`; it cannot be removed later (📖 R4.4.5).
   298	- **Incident lane:** PR label `incident`; the on-call approver is paged; security checks always run
   299	  (S10); only slow, unrelated tests are skipped [design]. Path filters on required checks may leave
   300	  them pending (⚠️ Options §8).
   301	
   302	**Example: Lambda timeouts at 2 a.m.** (Options §6)
   303	
   304	1. The incident-analysis session reads metrics and redacted logs and proposes: `timeout` 30 → 90 s,
   305	   `memorySize` 1024 → 2048, and a larger visibility timeout on the source queue.
   306	2. A second session, without prod identity, edits `serverless.yml`, runs `serverless diff` on
   307	   non-prod, and opens a PR labelled `incident` under the engineer's GitHub identity.
   308	3. The on-call approver reviews the diff (and catches it if the queue change is missing) and
   309	   approves. The engineer merges.
   310	4. The deploy workflow packages, previews prod, finds no risky change, and deploys with the OIDC role.
   311	5. Estimated 11–46 min (⚠️ unmeasured, Options §6) against a ≤ 30 min target, so measuring this lane
   312	   is a phase-1 task.
   313	
   314	**Audit evidence**
   315	
   316	- The PR: diff, approvers, check results, labels.
   317	- The run: prod-preview artifact and deploy log, exported because run retention is limited (G7).
   318	- CloudTrail events of the deploy role.
   319	- All linked by commit SHA and run ID (change record, layer 7).
   320	
   321	**Cost / value.** One approval for routine prod changes, two for risky ones, none in non-prod.
   322	Compliance gets four-eyes enforced by GitHub and a record per change. Open gap: on Team the
   323	reviewer sees the non-prod preview, not the prod diff.

   327	## Layer 4 — Supply chain for MCP servers and plugins
   328	
   329	**Principle**
   330	
   331	- An MCP server, plugin or hook is software that runs with the engineer's access (📖 R1.6.2,
   332	  R5.5.5). It is installed only from a reviewed catalogue, pinned to a version.
   333	- The catalogue is the inventory (P4c).
   334	- Review must be fast (S6: ≤ 5 working days), or people work around it.
   335	
   336	**On Papaya's stack**
   337	
   338	- **Plugins:**
   339	  - Papaya's private marketplace is a git repo with `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` (📖 R1.4.1);
   340	    sources pinned to a `ref` or `sha` (📖 R1.4.1b, link only).
   341	  - Managed: `strictKnownMarketplaces` = Papaya's marketplace only; `blockedMarketplaces`;
   342	    `enabledPlugins: false` to pull one plugin (📖 R1.4.2). The allowlist is checked before
   343	    download and at every session start (📖 R1.4.3).
   344	  - Client ≥ 2.1.179, which fixes a plugin-pin bypass (📖 R6.2.7).
   345	  - Plugin hooks run only if the plugin is force-enabled in managed `enabledPlugins` (📖 R1.5.4).
   346	    User-installed mods are blocked with `allowManagedModsOnly` (📖 R1.5.6).
   347	- **MCP servers:**
   348	  - "Approved catalogue" pattern: `allowedMcpServers` + `allowManagedMcpServersOnly: true`
   349	    (📖 R1.3.1). Without the lock, users can widen the list (📖 R1.3.4).
   350	  - Match by `serverUrl` or `serverCommand`, never `serverName`, which "is not a security control"
   351	    (📖 R1.3.3). The `serverCommand` argv pins an exact version, not `@latest` (⚠️ R6.4.4).
   352	  - `disableClaudeAiConnectors: true` (📖 R1.3.7).
   353	  - With MDM, `managed-mcp.json` gives exclusive control; it cannot be delivered server-managed
   354	    (📖 R1.3.2).
   355	- **Review checklist, one page per entry** [design]:
   356	  - publisher and licence; local (`stdio`) or remote (📖 R6.4.3);
   357	  - what it reads and where it sends data; a supplier-register entry with BAA/DPA status (⚠️ R5.6);
   358	  - authentication: no token passthrough (📖 R6.4.2);
   359	  - write tools (Slack, Linear, GitHub) only where needed (⚠️ R6.5 #6);
   360	  - pinned version, reviewer's name, review date.
   361	- **Known gaps:** server-managed settings cannot target groups (📖 R1.1.6, R1.4.4); changes to a
   362	  server's tool descriptions after approval are not covered (⚠️ research 6.6).
   363	
   364	**Audit evidence**
   365	
   366	- The catalogue file in git: each addition is a PR with an approver.
   367	- The supplier register.
   368	- OTel `claude_code.plugin_installed` / `plugin_loaded` (📖 R1.4.6). MCP tool usage needs
   369	  `OTEL_LOG_TOOL_DETAILS=1` (📖 R1.3.8); whether that logs tool inputs, which may hold data, is not
   370	  checked (⚠️), so it is off on laptops until checked.
   371	
   372	**Cost / value.** Engineers wait up to 5 days for a new tool, requested with a short form.
   373	Compliance gets an asset inventory and supplier records for every extension.

   377	## Layer 5 — CI/CD hardening
   378	
   379	**Principle**
   380	
   381	- CI is the only production writer, so it is the most valuable target.
   382	- No long-lived credentials; least-privilege, short-lived tokens per job.
   383	- Every third-party dependency of CI is pinned.
   384	- Code written by an agent goes through the same checks as code written by a person (S10).
   385	
   386	**On Papaya's stack**
   387	
   388	- **Credentials:**
   389	  - replace AWS keys and long-lived tokens in repository secrets with OIDC (📖 R3.4.1); anyone with
   390	    write access can read repository secrets (📖 R3.5.3b);
   391	  - `GITHUB_TOKEN` default "restricted"; each job declares `permissions:` (📖 R3.5.3);
   392	  - "Allow GitHub Actions to create and approve pull requests" stays **off** (📖 R3.3.3);
   393	  - Serverless License Key in SSM per account, read by the deploy role (sf ≥ 4.4.19, 📖 R4.4.8).
   394	- **Side paths to prod:** Pulumi ESC, Pulumi Deployments and Serverless Dashboard providers each
   395	  hold a path to AWS outside the GitHub gate (📖 R4.4.9). Default: not used for prod (⚠️ R4.6 #6).
   396	- **Pin CI dependencies:** workflow lockfile (`gh actions-lock`) or full SHA, enforced by the org
   397	  policy "Require actions to be pinned to a full-length commit SHA" (📖 R3.5.5).
   398	- **Protect the files that steer CI and agents** with CODEOWNERS: `.github/`, `CLAUDE.md`,
   399	  `CLAUDE.local.md`, `.claude/`, `.claude.json`, `.mcp.json`, `.husky/`, `.gitmodules`, `.ripgreprc`
   400	  (📖 R3.2.8; list ⚠️ R6.5 #5). CI validates the CODEOWNERS file, because invalid lines are skipped
   401	  silently (📖 R3.2.7).
   402	- **No privileged triggers with untrusted code:** no `pull_request_target` or `workflow_run` job
   403	  that checks out PR code (📖 R3.5.4).
   404	- **Agents in CI:**
   405	  - `claude-code-action` with a custom GitHub App limited to Contents, Issues and Pull requests
   406	    (📖 R1.7.8);
   407	  - authentication through Anthropic workload identity federation, not a personal OAuth token
   408	    (📖 R1.7.6);
   409	  - `--restricted`, explicit `--tools`, rules from a `--settings` file on the runner (D3; 📖 R6.9.1b).
   410	    Whether the action passes `--restricted` through `claude_args` is unverified (⚠️ research 6.9.4 Q5);
   411	  - the MCP set is fixed by the CI configuration, because project `.mcp.json` servers load without a
   412	    prompt in `-p` runs (📖 R1.3.5);
   413	  - never `environment: production` (⚠️ R3.6 #4);
   414	  - a managed-settings file written on a GitHub-hosted runner is advisory, because the job has
   415	    passwordless `sudo` (📖 R3.5.1);
   416	  - an app-authored PR needs 2 approvals, or a check with a pinned source app confirming one human
   417	    approver who did not trigger the run (⚠️ R3.3.4, R3.7 A3; pinned source 📖 R3.2.5).
   418	- **Required security checks on every PR to `main` (S10):** secret scanning, dependency check, SAST.
   419	  The products are **not researched** (G4). Researched checks: IAM Access Analyzer
   420	  `check-no-new-access` (📖 R2.8.1) and Pulumi policy packs (📖 R4.2.2).
   421	
   422	**Audit evidence**
   423	
   424	- Workflow files and the lockfile in git.
   425	- Org audit log events such as `org.update_actions_secret`, `repo.set_workflow_permission_can_approve_pr`,
   426	  `repository_ruleset.update` (📖 R3.3.3, R3.5.6).
   427	- Check results attached to each PR.
   428	- Inventory of repository secrets: zero cloud credentials (S2).
   429	
   430	**Cost / value.** A one-time migration of workflows to OIDC and pinned actions; slower updates of
   431	actions. Compliance gets S2 and the same guardrails for agent and human code.

   435	## Layer 6 — Data protection and prompt-injection defence
   436	
   437	**Principle**
   438	
   439	- **PHI never reaches the coding agent.** This is a hard boundary, enforced by what agent-usable
   440	  identities can read, not by instructions (owner decision, research 1.9).
   441	- **Other customer personal data** (e.g. a national ID number or bank account on its own) is not
   442	  covered by that decision (owner: "not extended", research 5.8). It is handled by data
   443	  minimisation and the default "agent reads metadata only" (GĐ-9).
   444	- Reading real data is an approved, tenant-scoped, time-boxed exception (GĐ-9).
   445	- Everything the agent reads from outside is untrusted: logs, tickets, PR text, web pages, MCP
   446	  results, uploaded documents.
   447	- Non-prod holds synthetic data only (GĐ-14).
   448	
   449	**On Papaya's stack**
   450	
   451	- **Close the paths by identity.** Research 1.11.4 lists 17 ways PHI can reach the agent (logs,
   452	  SQL, S3, queues, tickets, uploaded documents, exec, replay…). The main control is that
   453	  agent-usable identities have **no data-plane read** on PHI stores (⚠️ R2.9 #4, research 1.11.4):
   454	  - SCP deny on claim buckets and queues (layer 1);
   455	  - no Aurora credentials for engineers' agents; no prod exec (ECS Exec, `kubectl exec`) and no
   456	    `lambda:InvokeFunction` for agent-usable roles [design; exact IAM action for ECS Exec not
   457	    researched];
   458	  - logs redacted at source, raw payloads in a separate log group that `ProdReadMetadata` cannot
   459	    read [design];
   460	  - alerts carry IDs and links, not payloads [design].
   461	- **The real-data exception** [design]. It narrows GĐ-9 for PHI: GĐ-9 allows an agent to read real
   462	  data case by case, but the Team plan is not shown as covered by Anthropic's BAA (⚠️ R1.11.3.4)
   463	  and the owner keeps the agent outside PHI scope (research 1.9). So:
   464	  - a request names tenant, purpose, store and time window; the data owner and an approver ≠
   465	    requester approve it;
   466	  - a JIT data-read role, 1 h; scoping it to one tenant is intended through session tags (📖 R2.1.8),
   467	    but tags come from user attributes, so per-request tenant scope is unresolved (⚠️);
   468	  - the human reads in a terminal without an agent; the agent gets only a redacted summary;
   469	  - for non-PHI personal data, the same request path applies by default; whether the agent may read
   470	    it directly is an owner decision (open).
   471	  - Enterprise + BAA is considered only for a separate environment if a real need appears (research 1.9).
   472	- **Partner-uploaded claim documents (A7b).** Never open a raw partner document in an agent session.
   473	  Reproduce bugs with synthetic documents; if a real one is needed, de-identify it first [design].
   474	  The document is both PHI and an injection vector (research 1.11.4 L6).
   475	- **Injection controls** (each lowers frequency; layers 1 and 3 limit impact):
   476	  - Manual mode by default (layer 2); the auto-mode classifier missed 17 % of 52 real overeager
   477	    actions (📖 R6.3.5);
   478	  - narrow sandbox egress; allowing a broad domain such as `github.com` is a documented
   479	    exfiltration path (📖 R6.3.7);
   480	  - the incident-analysis session has no edit tools, so a poisoned log line cannot edit code. It can
   481	    still run Bash: it could send data the session has read to an allowed domain, or use any role in
   482	    the SSO cache. Egress limits and the "no standing write" rule bound that;
   483	  - shared knowledge in `CLAUDE.md` under CODEOWNERS; auto memory is outside protected paths unless
   484	    `--restricted` (📖 R6.3.11, D3).
   485	- **Tenant isolation:** one tenant per data-read grant; a session that read one tenant's data does
   486	  not write to shared places (PRs, tickets, chat) without review (⚠️ R6.5 #8, process rule). Tenants
   487	  are assumed logically separated in shared accounts (GĐ-3, unconfirmed).
   488	- **Non-prod:** no copies from prod to non-prod (S11); periodic scan of non-prod stores (tool: G4).
   489	- **Local stores:** transcripts and memory hold what the agent read (research 1.11.4). Set
   490	  `cleanupPeriodDays` (the HIPAA sample uses 30, 📖 R5.5.4).
   491	- **Cross-border (Vietnam example):** processing personal data collected in Vietnam on a platform
   492	  abroad counts as a transfer (📖 R5.4.5). If agent flows carry such data, they update Papaya's
   493	  transfer impact assessment (⚠️ R5.7 #4, needs Papaya legal).
   494	
   495	**Audit evidence**
   496	
   497	- SCP and IAM text: the written access rule for sensitive data (📖 R5.4.3, Decree 356 Art. 4(2);
   498	  ⚠️ R5.7 #5).
   499	- Data-read requests (requester, approver, tenant, window) and CloudTrail of the data-read role.
   500	- Planted-injection test reports (layer 8).
   501	- Non-prod scan results (S11).
   502	
   503	**Cost / value.** Engineers debug with redacted data and synthetic documents; the rare real-data
   504	read needs a request. Compliance gets "minimum necessary" (📖 R1.11.1.8) enforced by IAM, and a
   505	record for every exception (S9).

   509	## Layer 7 — Observability, audit, incident response and break-glass
   510	
   511	**Principle**
   512	
   513	- One evidence chain per production change: who proposed, who approved, what was approved, what was
   514	  applied, when. Tamper-evident.
   515	- Every prod alert goes to one routed channel with a named on-call, including on-call approvers.
   516	- Unapproved change is detected: drift and reconciliation are compliance signals.
   517	- Break-glass is time-boxed, approved before use, and reconciled after.
   518	
   519	**On Papaya's stack**
   520	
   521	- **Audit stores:**
   522	  - an organization CloudTrail to a log-archive account with integrity validation (📖 R2.6.2,
   523	    R2.6.4, R2.6.5). Retention: approval and change records ≥ 6 years (📖 R5.2.9); whether raw logs
   524	    need 6 years is for Papaya's compliance owner (⚠️ R5.7 #2). Data events only on PHI buckets, prod
   525	    Lambda invoke and prod SQS, because of cost (📖 R2.6.3); these make the archive a PHI-bearing
   526	    store (⚠️ research 1.11.4 L15). CloudTrail Lake is closed to new customers (📖 R2.6.6);
   527	  - the GitHub org audit log keeps 180 days, so it is exported (📖 R3.5.6);
   528	  - alerts on `repository_ruleset.update`, `environment.update_protection_rule`,
   529	    `environment.remove_protection_rule`, `environment.delete`, `protected_branch.policy_override`
   530	    (📖 R3.5.6, R3.6 #1). Limits: `environment.update_protection_rule` covers API changes; UI changes
   531	    to the branch policy may leave no event, and a deleted `production` environment is recreated
   532	    without protection by the next workflow (⚠️ R3.1.4). Hence the configuration comparison job (layer 8);
   533	  - Aurora: SQL over a normal connection is likely invisible to CloudTrail (⚠️ R2.7.1); Database
   534	    Activity Streams, an alert on lost-record events, and the stream treated as PHI (📖 R2.7.2;
   535	    Serverless v2 support ⚠️);
   536	  - Claude Code OTel without prompt content (layer 2).
   537	- **Change record [design]:** one record per prod change, keyed by commit SHA or JIT request ID,
   538	  linking PR, approvals, run ID, preview artifact and CloudTrail events. Monthly reconciliation:
   539	  every prod write event must match an approved record, approved before apply (S3). The actor is
   540	  `onBehalfOf.userId` where present, otherwise session name plus directory lookup (📖 R2.5.1;
   541	  ⚠️ R2.5.3 for member-account events). Detecting a PR approval that an agent gave through its
   542	  engineer's login is not possible with the researched data sources (⚠️ open).
   543	- **Drift detection:** scheduled `pulumi preview --refresh --expect-no-changes` or
   544	  `pulumi refresh --preview-only` (📖 R4.1.1, R4.3.1, R4.3.2); CloudFormation drift detection for
   545	  Serverless stacks, which checks only explicitly set properties (📖 R4.4.6). Auto-remediation stays
   546	  off, since it runs an unreviewed `pulumi up --refresh` (📖 R4.3.2). Each finding gets an actor and a ticket.
   547	- **On-call (phase 1 minimum):** a named schedule with primary and backup approvers, 24/7, including
   548	  a platform approver; one alert route for prod. Paging and alert tools are not researched (G5).
   549	- **Break-glass = the JIT fallback (D2', all steps [design] unless marked):**
   550	
   551	  | Step | Rule |
   552	  |---|---|
   553	  | When | Only if the PR path cannot be used: the operation is not in IaC and has no runbook, GitHub/Actions is down, or the pipeline is broken. The approver refuses JIT for anything expressible in IaC while the pipeline works, and records why. |
   554	  | Request | Exact command list, service scope, reason, incident link. |
   555	  | Approval | On-call approver ≠ requester, before the grant. |
   556	  | Grant | `InfraOperator-<service>`, session 1 h (📖 R2.1.4). Deleting the assignment likely stops only new role sessions (⚠️ R2.4.2, R2.9 #3); ending the portal session does not end role sessions (📖 R2.4.3). An immediate stop needs the revocation SCP (📖 R2.4.4). |
   557	  | Execution | The human types the commands in a terminal with no agent; the agent may propose them from its analysis session (D2, process rule). |
   558	  | After | A job compares CloudTrail writes in the window with the list. A codify PR within a fixed deadline; drift detection until it merges. |
   559	  | Identity Center outage | Emergency direct SAML federation to roles, built and tested in advance, with its own approval and alerting (📖 R2.1.11; ⚠️ R2.9 #2). |
   560	
   561	  Metrics with review thresholds: JIT grants per month; JIT used for changes that should have gone
   562	  through a PR; windows with writes outside the list; codify PRs past deadline (Options §7). When a
   563	  threshold is passed, make the PR lane faster or move the operation to a runbook; do not widen JIT.
   564	- **Roadmap:** pre-approved runbooks for repeated non-IaC operations, if SSM Automation's approval
   565	  step meets three conditions (Options §2; source outside `02-research.md`, G6); break-glass from an
   566	  environment without the agent installed (D2, ⚠️).
   567	
   568	**Audit evidence**
   569	
   570	- CloudTrail with digest validation; exported GitHub audit log.
   571	- Change records and the monthly reconciliation report (S3; S8: evidence for a random change in
   572	  ≤ 30 min).
   573	- JIT request records, reconciliation results, codify PRs.
   574	- Drift reports and their tickets.
   575	- On-call schedule and approval-wait times (S7).
   576	
   577	**Cost / value.** The incident lane is slower than direct access (estimated 11–46 min against
   578	2–20 min, Options §6, unmeasured), and JIT needs a written command list. Compliance gets approval
   579	before apply even in incidents, and detection when something slips through.

   583	## Layer 8 — Governance and rollout
   584	
   585	**Principle**
   586	
   587	- Every control has an owner, a metric and a review date.
   588	- Policies live in git and change through PRs, like code.
   589	- Access is never removed before its replacement works; otherwise the rule breaks in the first
   590	  incident.
   591	- Controls are tested against realistic attacks, not only reviewed on paper.
   592	
   593	**On Papaya's stack**
   594	
   595	- **Ownership:**
   596	
   597	  | Area | Owner |
   598	  |---|---|
   599	  | Framework, managed settings, MCP/plugin catalogue, metrics | DevSecOps |
   600	  | Deploy workflows, rulesets, Pulumi/Serverless pipelines | Platform team |
   601	  | Evidence review, supplier register, ISO Statement of Applicability | Compliance |
   602	  | CODEOWNERS per domain | Engineering leads |
   603	  | On-call schedule (incl. approvers) | Engineering management |
   604	
   605	- **Policy as code:** managed settings, the MCP/plugin catalogue, SCPs, trust policies, CODEOWNERS
   606	  and deploy workflows are files under CODEOWNERS. GitHub settings that are not files (rulesets,
   607	  environments) are compared with an expected copy by a scheduled job (⚠️ R3.6 #1; API not
   608	  researched, G8).
   609	- **Admins:** few GitHub admins and holders of "edit repository rules"; quarterly review; alerts as
   610	  in layer 7.
   611	- **Metrics** (framing §5): S1 standing prod write = 0 · S2 cloud credentials in GitHub secrets = 0
   612	  · S3 unmatched prod changes = 0 and approvals after apply = 0 · S4 approval wait ≤ 1 h · S5
   613	  machines with the policy = 100 % · S6 tools outside the catalogue = 0, requests handled ≤ 5 working
   614	  days · S7 incident approval wait ≤ 15 min · S8 evidence in ≤ 30 min · S9 data reads without
   615	  approval = 0 · S10 PRs with checks = 100 % · S11 prod copies in non-prod = 0.
   616	- **Rollout** (≈ 90 days, GĐ-12 unconfirmed). Priorities from framing round 1 Q8: (1) remove
   617	  long-lived CI credentials, (2) narrow standing prod access, (3) MCP/plugin catalogue; the on-call
   618	  approver list comes first or together with (2):
   619	
   620	  | Phase | Days | Items |
   621	  |---|---|---|
   622	  | 1 | 0–30 | OIDC for all deploy workflows; remove AWS keys and long-lived tokens. Rulesets and CODEOWNERS on `main`. On-call approver schedule (primary + backup + platform). Minimal JIT fallback (request, approval, 1 h assignment). **Then** remove standing prod-write assignments. Managed settings baseline. MCP/plugin catalogue v1. Measure the incident PR lane on one real service (target ≤ 30 min). Start verifying the SSM runbook option (Options §7). |
   623	  | 2 | 30–60 | SCP data-plane deny; drift detection; audit-log export, change records and reconciliation; Aurora activity streams; JIT reconciliation job. |
   624	  | 3 | 60–90 | Planted-injection test on the real configuration (📖 R6.1.1, R6.1.5; ⚠️ R6.5 #12); auto-mode non-prod environment (D1 c); separate OS user or container for prod-reading sessions; first ISO 27001 evidence pack; 90-day review of D1–D3. |
   625	
   626	- **Exceptions:** a deviation from this framework is a ticket with an owner, a reason and an
   627	  expiry date.
   628	- **Standards mapping:** HIPAA Security Rule sections are cited from the text (📖 R5.2). ISO/IEC
   629	  27001:2022 Annex A control IDs are recalled, not read, and must be checked against a licensed copy
   630	  (⚠️ R5.1). The "named human approves" rule is Papaya's chosen way to meet change-management and
   631	  segregation-of-duties controls, not a literal requirement (⚠️ R5.7 #1).
   632	- **Out of scope:** security of Papaya's production AI agents (A7a); noted as a follow-up.
   633	
   634	**Audit evidence**
   635	
   636	- Git history of every policy file.
   637	- Monthly metric report; 90-day review minutes; exception register.
   638	- Planted-injection test reports.
   639	
   640	**Cost / value.** A small, steady overhead for DevSecOps and the platform team. Compliance gets a
   641	managed system with owners and review dates.
